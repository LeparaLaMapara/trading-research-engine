## This week in plain words (model synthesis)

**Connections across fields**

* The two cards come from different fields, statistics (W44-1) and execution theory (W44-2), but share one idea: a measured gain can come from something other than the skill being claimed, and the way to separate the two is to compare against a control that keeps the free part and scrambles the skill part. In W44-1 the free part is simply being long while ETH rose, so the block shuffle keeps time spent long and scrambles the timing. In W44-2 the free part is the spread saved by waiting with a limit order. Random-direction orders measure that saving, and signal-signed orders reveal whether the signal's timing gives it back through adverse selection. Both cards say the engine's current baselines (random signs; always crossing the spread) leave this confound uncontrolled.
* Both cards point the same way about trend signals. W44-1 suspects E07's apparent timing skill is mostly exposure. W44-2 expects passive orders to fill mainly when the trend signal is wrong. If W44-1 finds real timing in E07, adverse selection in W44-2 should be stronger on ETHUSD. If W44-1 finds no timing, the signal-signed and random-direction results in W44-2 should look alike on ETHUSD. Running both on ETHUSD lets each result serve as a consistency check on the other.
* W44-1 turns into a better promotion test. That connects it to the two top-priority queue items, W40-1 (a reproducible audit protocol for trading policies) and W42-3 (anytime-valid promotion tests). Only their titles are in the queue, so this link is a fit of purpose, not a demonstrated overlap of method.

**What this should change**

* E07's pass against 10,000 random-sign runs should be downgraded from 'evidence of trend-timing skill' to 'evidence of something, possibly just long exposure during the 2018–2022 rise'. The random-sign null cannot tell those apart. Nothing has been tested yet, so this lowers confidence; it does not overturn the result.
* The pass criteria and audit A01, which rely on the random-sign null, should be treated as too lenient for any strategy that spends most of its time on one side of a market that trended. Strategies that passed this way deserve the same suspicion as E07.
* Even if E07 passes the block shuffle, that would show gross timing skill concentrated in a past period that has since faded. It would not show the strategy is profitable after the roughly 20% a year cost of holding crypto longs.
* The engine's assumption that entries and exits must pay the full spread is unproven, but so is the alternative. The card itself names adverse selection as the likely way the limit-order saving disappears, so there is no reason yet to believe execution costs can be cut.

**Gaps**

* The block shuffle in W44-1 keeps time spent long but not when the strategy was long. Swap rates have changed over time, so the cost side is matched only approximately. The card runs on gross returns to sidestep this, which means a pass says nothing about net profitability.
* No card says which other promoted or candidate strategies besides E07 and E08 passed through the random-sign null and would need re-auditing if W44-1 confirms the concern.
* W44-2 needs a realistic model of when a resting limit order on the IC Markets quote would actually fill. The cards do not say whether intraday quote data at fine enough resolution is available to simulate this, or how the broker's quote differs from a real order book.
* Nothing this week addresses whether E07's apparent edge has decayed for good, or how the engine should handle strategies whose evidence is concentrated in a single past period.
* The queue items W40-1, W42-3 and regime-trend-6 have no cards, only titles, so any link to them is provisional. regime-trend-6 (drawdown-controlled portfolio selection) sits at priority 0 and connects to nothing this week.

**Suggested next steps**

* `2026-W44-1`: This is the cheapest card (cost 1) with the highest information score (3), and it directly tests whether the engine's best-looking result, E07, is real timing or just exposure. Use at least 2,000 shuffles, run the full sample and 2018–2022 separately, and include E08 as a no-edge check. Before trusting a 'no timing' verdict, confirm the test's power with oracle strategies of known edge, as the paper does. A 20-day trend rule over nine years has relatively few position changes to shuffle, so the test may simply lack the strength to detect a real effect.
* `2026-W42-3`: This is tied for the highest priority in the queue. If W44-1 shows the random-sign null is too lenient, the promotion rule needs rebuilding anyway, and an anytime-valid test, one that stays correct however often results are checked, is the natural frame for a stricter rule. Its content is known only from its title, so the first step is to read it and write a proper card.
* `2026-W44-2`: This is moderately cheap and fully specified with clear failure conditions. Run it only with the signal-signed arm included from the start. The random-direction saving alone would overstate the benefit, because adverse selection on trend signals is the expected failure mode. Starting with ETHUSD lets the result be read alongside W44-1.

## Idea cards

### `2026-W44-1` Indistinguishable from Chance: Auditing LLM-Generated Trading Strategies Against Exposure-Matched Nulls

statistics, [doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-11272412/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11272412/v1), read: full (open-access PDF). **priority 6.0**

* **Concept:** Exposure-matched permutation null (E4 in the paper). Cut the position series at every change of position, put the blocks in a random order 200 times, and compare gross Sharpe. The one-sided p-value is (1 + number of shuffles with Sharpe at or above the strategy's) / 201. Run on gross returns because merged blocks trade less. Power is calibrated with oracles of known edge.
* **Market analogue:** Direct. The engine's pass criteria and audit A01 use a random-sign null. The Ethereum 20-day trend (E07) beat 10,000 random-sign runs, but all its edge came in 2018 to 2022, an era when ETH rose strongly. A long-biased trend rule there could beat random signs on exposure alone. The block shuffle separates the two. Swap cost is driven by time spent long, which the shuffle preserves (apart from swap rates changing over time), so the comparison is fair even though crypto longs pay about 20% a year and shorts pay nothing.
* **Claim:** A strategy that beats buy-and-hold or a symmetric random-sign null may still contain no timing skill: its edge can come from exposure, meaning time spent long, short or flat. Shuffling a strategy's own blocks of constant position keeps that exposure and the block lengths and destroys only the timing. In the paper, positive-control oracles show this exposure-matched test detects a gross timing Sharpe of about 0.5–0.6 in 63–80% of runs and about 1.0 in every run. Every apparent improvement in the LLM strategies (a robustness prompt, a larger model, the gold market) turned out to be exposure, not timing.
* **Hypothesis:** Rebuild E07's ETHUSD daily position series (2017–2026, broker D1 bars). Its gross Sharpe will NOT exceed the 99th percentile of at least 2,000 shuffles of its own blocks of constant position. That would mean its random-sign pass reflected long exposure in the 2018–2022 rise, not trend timing. If instead its gross Sharpe beats the shuffle 99th percentile over the full sample and in 2018–2022, the edge was genuine timing that has since decayed. Run the same check on E08 (USDSGD) as a secondary diagnostic; no edge is expected there.
* **Test:** Diagnostic and method calibration, history only, no slot, no rule can be promoted or reinstated by any outcome. (1) Rebuild E07 (ETHUSD) and E08 (USDSGD) daily position series from held broker D1 bars. Verify that the trade count and gross P&L match the recorded E07 and E08 backtests exactly before going further. Report the number of constant-position blocks per series and per era. (2) Cut each series at every position change, flat periods included. Generate 10,000 block shuffles and compute gross Sharpe. p = (1 + number of shuffles >= actual) / 10,001. Run three nulls: full sample 2017 to 2026, within-era 2018 to 2022-09 only, and within-era 2022-09 to 2026. The decisive statistic is E07 within-era 2018 to 2022-09. The others, and all of E08, are descriptive. (3) Power: build oracles that keep E07's own block lengths and long/short/flat exposure, but assign each block's sign correctly with probability p in {0.55, 0.60, 0.70, 0.80}, 100 seeds each. Report detection rate at the 1% level against planted gross timing Sharpe. (4) Negative control: 100 exposure-matched random-sign assignments. The rejection rate at 1% must fall between 0% and 3%, or the harness is faulty. (5) Verdict rules, fixed in advance. If oracles with timing Sharpe of about 1.0 are detected in fewer than 80% of runs at 1%, the result is INCONCLUSIVE (underpowered) and the method is NOT added to pass criteria. If power is adequate and E07 within-era p < 0.01, record 'genuine timing in 2018 to 2022, since decayed'. If p >= 0.01, record 'random-sign pass was exposure'. (6) Adoption: if power is adequate, add 'beats its own exposure-matched block-shuffle null at the 99th percentile, gross, within each half' to the pass criteria for any candidate whose time-weighted net exposure exceeds 25% long or short, alongside the random-sign null. Log the run as diagnostic, outside N.
> For each LLM audit, the position series is cut at every change of position, and the resulting blocks are concatenated in a random order, 200 times.
> Each shuﬄe has exactly the time spent long, short and ﬂat of the original strategy and the same blocks; only the timing diﬀers.

### `2026-W44-2` Optimal execution with limit and market orders

foundations, [doi:10.1080/14697688.2015.1032543](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2015.1032543), read: abstract. **priority 3.0**

* **Concept:** Optimal execution of a large parent order with a choice of limit or market child orders and of limit-order depth, measured against the Almgren-Chriss volume schedule.
* **Market analogue:** The engine's entries and exits on daily-signal rules, which currently cross the spread with market orders. The 'inventory schedule' maps to 'be filled by roughly the signal time'. 'Earning the spread' maps to resting a limit at the near touch (bid for a buy, ask for a sell) on the IC Markets quote. The analogy breaks in three ways. Our 0.01-lot orders have no market impact, so the Almgren-Chriss impact saving does not exist here. The counterparty is the broker's quote rather than a lit queue. And a passive order on a trend signal fills mostly when price moves against the signal (adverse selection) and misses when the signal is right.
* **Claim:** For an investor executing a large order to a volume schedule (Almgren-Chriss), mixing limit orders, which earn the spread, with market orders, which keep inventory on schedule, beats the pure Almgren-Chriss price by about one to two and a half spreads per share on average.
* **Hypothesis:** On EURGBP, AUDCAD and ETHUSD, entering at the 22:00-23:00 UTC daily signal time with a buy limit at the bid (sell limit at the ask) has a lower expected implementation shortfall than a market order. The order waits up to 15 minutes and then falls back to a market order. Shortfall is measured against the mid at decision time and includes the cost of the fallback fills. The saving must be at least 0.3 of the median spread per side, averaged over random-direction decisions. Falsified if the mean saving is at or below zero, or if the saving shrinks to zero once orders are signed by an actual trend signal (adverse selection).
* **Test:** Cost-measurement study, not a strategy; it adds nothing to the N=307 rule count. It uses held top-of-book ticks only and reports the span of days available per instrument before any result. Instruments: EURGBP, AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD (ETHUSD reported separately, descriptive only). Decision times: 22:30 UTC each trading day, plus a control at 14:00 UTC to separate the post-rollover spread effect. Each decision gets a random sign, drawn from a fixed seed. At decision time t0 + 1.1 s (measured latency), place a buy limit at the bid (sell limit at the ask) observed at t0. It counts as filled only if the opposite touch trades strictly through the limit by at least one tick within 15 minutes. Otherwise fill at the touch at t0 + 15 min. Baselines: (a) market order at t0 + 1.1 s; (b) market order at t0 + 15 min. Shortfall is measured against the t0 mid, and every leg is marked to the t0 + 15 min mid so that horizons match. Primary statistic: mean paired saving versus BOTH baselines, in units of the median spread, with a block bootstrap over days (10,000 draws). Pass only if the lower 99% bound of the saving is above zero against both baselines in at least 3 of 4 FX pairs, at both decision times. Secondary: the same with orders signed by the 20-day trend, where a saving cancelled to at most zero falsifies use on trend entries. If it passes, run 20 live EURGBP limit probes in the E04 style. They check fill rate and fill price versus the touch against the simulator; a fill rate more than 10 points below the simulated rate voids the result.
> We develop an optimal execution policy for an investor seeking to execute a large order using limit and market orders.
> The different strategies considered by the investor outperform the Almgren-Chriss price with an average savings per share of about one to two and a half times the spread.
