## This week in plain words (model synthesis)

**Connections across fields**

* The statistics card (2026-W46-1) and the physics card (2026-W46-2) build the same kind of control experiment. Each one scrambles the data in a way that keeps the boring structure and destroys only the effect being tested. W46-1 keeps how many times the rule switches between long, short and flat, which also fixes roughly how long it sits long or short, and then randomises when the switches happen. What is left to test is timing skill. W46-2 time-shifts each coin against the others. Each coin keeps its own memory, and only the coins' influence on one another is destroyed. Both ask how much of an apparent effect survives once you match everything except the mechanism you claim. That is the physicist's 'surrogate data' idea applied to trading rules.
* The physics card's own caveat carries back into the statistics card. The W46-2 time-shift also wipes out the common factor (all crypto moving together), so a gap between the real data and the scrambled twin can come from that shared movement rather than from one coin leading another. W46-1 faces the same risk in another form. If its reshuffles do not preserve something real, such as the long-heavy mix and the financing cost that comes with it, the null will credit or blame timing for something that is really composition. That is why W46-1 insists on carrying spread and swap through every reshuffle.
* The seasonality card (2026-W46-3) uses a calendar circular-shift null. This belongs to the same family: shift the calendar labels, keep the return series intact. Three of this week's four cards therefore converge on one method question, which the queued 2026-W44-1 ('exposure-matched nulls') also raises: is the engine's standard random-sign null matched closely enough to what each rule actually does?
* The implied-accuracy card (2026-W46-4) and W46-1 share a cost point. Spread per trade and the one-sided swap (crypto longs pay about 20% a year, shorts pay nothing) set a hurdle that any timing skill must clear. W46-1 builds that hurdle into the null. W46-4 states it as a minimum hit rate. The red team judged W46-4 to be only a re-expression of net Sharpe, which the engine already computes.

**What this should change**

* Treat the current random-sign null at the 99th percentile as possibly too lenient for rules with lopsided long/short/flat mixes, until tested. This is a hypothesis (W46-1 part 1), not a result. Nothing this week shows it leaks. But if it does, earlier passes such as E07's 2018–2022 ETHUSD trend edge may partly reflect which side the rule sat on, not when it traded.
* Lower the prior that cross-coin lead-lag among ETHUSD, ADAUSD, BCHUSD and LTCUSD is a usable edge. The physics source's own market figure (interaction share around 1–3%) is below the card's 5% gate. Its classic stock-market cause, assets closing at different times, does not exist in synchronously closed 24/7 crypto bars. Under the 2-position limit the basket cannot be held anyway. Any tradeable remnant overlaps the failed pooled ML work and the E16 scoreboard.
* Lower the prior on calendar seasonality in the affordable FX crosses. The source paper itself found currency-sector seasonality not significant. Our roughly 21 annual cycles of out-of-sample data cannot detect an effect small enough to fit that null result.
* Do not add implied directional accuracy as a separate screening gate. Per the red team, it restates net Sharpe and cannot fail informatively against rules that have already failed.

**Gaps**

* No card tests whether the 'transition-matched' and 'exposure-matched' nulls (W46-1 vs W44-1) actually differ in practice. We only know they aim at the same problem.
* W46-1 does not say how event eligibility is defined, for example whether a rule may switch on any bar or only on signal dates. The null's strictness depends on that choice.
* Inconsistency in W46-3: it proposes an out-of-sample window of 2005–2026, but its own market analogue says broker D1 history starts in 2014. The card is killed, but the same data-length limit applies to any slow-calendar idea.
* Nothing this week separates the crypto common factor from true coin-to-coin influence. W46-2's twin removes both together, and no card offers a null that keeps the shared movement while breaking lead-lag.
* Two queue items (2026-W40-1 XAI audit protocol and regime-trend-6 drawdown-controlled portfolios) have only titles here. There is not enough information to link them or rank them against this week's cards.
* This week contains no new candidate edge, only audit and method cards. The engine's search for positive timing skill made no progress.

**Suggested next steps**

Simulate no-skill rules whose switch counts match E07 and the E10 turn-of-month BTCUSD rule, and measure how often the current random-sign null wrongly passes them. If the false-pass rate is above the pre-registered 1.5%, the engine's main yardstick needs replacing, and that matters more than any single strategy. Only then run part 2 on E07.
* `2026-W44-1`: Its title (exposure-matched nulls for auditing LLM-generated strategies) points at the same question as W46-1. Before spending a separate slot, decide whether it can be folded into the W46-1 calibration as a second null design, so both are compared on the same no-skill simulations instead of tested twice.

## Idea cards

### `2026-W46-1` Agent Policy-Value Audit: Separating Transition Composition from Event Selection in Financial LLM Agents

statistics, [doi:10.1145/3858063.3858879](https://doi.org/10.1145/3858063.3858879), read: abstract. **priority 3.0**

* **Concept:** A permutation null matched on transitions: fixed counts of each ordered action-change type, reassigned across eligible events. Deployment value is split into a composition benchmark plus selection value.
* **Market analogue:** Events are the D1 bars (or signal dates) where the rule could act. Action changes are the rule's flat→long, long→flat, flat→short and short→flat transitions. The composition benchmark is the net P&L of randomly placed trades with the same transition counts, and therefore roughly the same time long and time short. Selection value is the timing edge the engine actually wants. Costs must be carried through each reshuffle: the spread per transition plus the lopsided swap (crypto longs pay about 20% a year, shorts pay nothing). That means composition includes the financing burden of a long-biased mix.
* **Claim:** Testing a trading agent's paired return against zero mixes up two things: what its mix of position changes would earn on any events (for example, being long in a market that drifts up) and genuine skill in choosing which events to act on. The proposed audit keeps the count of each ordered action change fixed (flat to long, long to flat, and so on) and reshuffles those changes randomly across the eligible events. The average payoff of the reshuffles is a 'composition benchmark'. Observed value minus that benchmark is 'selection value'. In the paper's semi-synthetic tests, the zero-centred test credited passive exposure as skill in 11.6% of no-skill runs, against 5.3% for the transition-matched audit. On 723 real earnings events, a +15.2 bps/event gross value split into +25.8 composition and -10.6 selection.
* **Hypothesis:** (1) Calibration: on ETHUSD and BTCUSD broker D1 bars from 2017 to 2026, simulate no-skill rules whose transition counts match E07 and the E10 turn-of-month BTCUSD rule, with entries placed at random. The engine's current random-sign null at the 99th percentile will pass these no-skill rules more often than the nominal 1%. The transition-matched null will pass them at or below 1%. (2) Applied: E07's ETHUSD 20-day trend has a selection value (net of spread and swap) that does not exceed the 99th percentile of 10,000 transition-matched reshuffles in either half, 2018 to 2022 or September 2022 to 2026. That would mean even its 2018 to 2022 edge was mostly composition, not timing. Part (1) fails if the random-sign null's false-pass rate on no-skill rules is at or below 1.5% across 2,000 simulations. Part (2) is falsified if E07's selection value exceeds the 99th percentile in the 2018 to 2022 half.
* **Test:** Run this as a calibration of the engine's pass criteria. It uses no trading slot and should be merged with the queued 'Indistinguishable from Chance' item so that only one entry exists. Data: broker D1 bars for ETHUSD, BTCUSD and USDSGD, 2017 to 2026, with measured spread and swap charged by direction (crypto longs about 20% a year, shorts 0; USDSGD +0.44 long and -1.34 short points a lot a day). Eligible bars exclude the 21:00 UTC rollover for USDSGD. Profiles: take the realised holding intervals (direction and duration) of E07 (ETHUSD), E10's turn-of-month BTCUSD rule and E08 (USDSGD). For each profile, generate 2,000 no-skill position series by placing the same intervals at random non-overlapping start bars. Score each series net of costs against the engine's current random-sign null at the 99th percentile, using 2,000 draws and the same direction-specific swap. Report the false-pass rate with a 95% binomial interval, pooled and separately for 2017 to 2022 and September 2022 to 2026. Pre-registered decision: if the lower bound of that interval exceeds 1% for any profile, add an interval-matched null at the 99th percentile as an extra pass criterion for every future rule. Otherwise leave the criteria unchanged and close the idea. Descriptive only, with no demotion consequence: report the interval-matched selection-value percentile for E07 by half and for the E10 turn-of-month BTCUSD rule.
> An agent that frequently changes positions from flat to long can earn a positive paired return from an upward-drifting event pool even when it selects events at random.
> We propose the Agent Policy-Value Audit, which holds fixed the observed count of each ordered action-change type and randomly reassigns them across eligible events.

### `2026-W46-2` Telling coordination from coincidence: a no-interaction twin for collective systems

physics, [arXiv:2611.10367v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10367v1), read: abstract. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Emergence and collective order in statistical physics (magnets, oscillators, flocks, fish schools). The quantity is the share of the group variable's forecastable structure that comes from member interactions rather than from independent members. The construction relates S to KL divergence for Gaussian forecasts.
* **Market analogue:** Each mapping:
- Members: the individual instruments, here the four affordable crypto CFDs ETHUSD, ADAUSD, BCHUSD and LTCUSD.
- Group variable: their equal-weight log-return basket.
- No-interaction twin: the same four return series, each randomly time-shifted relative to the others, so every cross-coin lead-lag is destroyed and each coin's own autocorrelation is kept.
- Interaction share S: the extra basket predictability that comes from cross-coin lead-lag or contagion.

Where the analogy could be wrong:
- Crypto co-movement is mostly a common factor, not interaction between coins. The time-shift twin removes the common factor too, so a 'real minus twin' gap may reflect contemporaneous correlation inflating the basket's variance rather than tradeable lead-lag.
- The paper's own market result (S about 1-3%) suggests the effect is far too small to pay this broker's spreads.
* **Claim:** The paper defines an interaction share S: the fraction of a no-interaction twin's forecast error that the real group law removes. The twin is the same members with every interaction removed. For linear forecasts S reduces to a 'memory gap' computed from two autocorrelation curves, with no twin to fit. In US stock markets S is small, about 1-3%, and this held up in pre-registered tests on unseen data. Read depth is the abstract only: no horizons, assets or trading results are given.
* **Hypothesis:** This is a diagnostic gate, not a strategy. On broker D1 bars (21:00 UTC close) for ETHUSD, ADAUSD, BCHUSD and LTCUSD, compute the memory-gap interaction share S for the equal-weight basket, using lags 1-5 and a linear forecast. The hypothesis that would justify a slot is S >= 0.05 in BOTH halves (2018-2022 and Sep 2022-2026). It is falsified if S < 0.05 in either half. That includes the paper's own reported market range of about 1-3%, which we would treat as untradeable here.

If, and only if, the gate passes, a second pre-registered hypothesis would follow. A next-day ETHUSD position in the sign of the basket's interaction-only forecast, short side preferred because crypto shorts pay no swap, would beat a random-sign null at the 99th percentile after measured spread and swap. The account allows at most 2 positions, so the 4-coin basket itself cannot be held; only one or two legs can be traded.
* **Test:** Data: held broker D1 bars for the four coins.

Step 1 (no slot spent, a pure diagnostic):
- Compute the basket autocorrelation curve, lags 1-5.
- Build 1,000 twins by independently circular-shifting each coin's return series by random offsets of at least 30 days. Compute the twin basket autocorrelation curve for each.
- Form S as the paper's memory gap. The abstract states S is computable from the two curves, but the exact formula is not given in the abstract, so obtain it from the paper's public code.
- Null: S drawn from the shifted-twin distribution, which has expectation zero.
- Failure: S below 0.05, or not above the 99th percentile of the twin distribution, in either half. Expected outcome: failure, which costs nothing and closes the idea.

Step 2 (runs only if Step 1 passes, and uses the 1 remaining P1 slot or a new programme slot):
- Backtest the ETHUSD sign rule on history with measured spread (about 3 USD/ETH), long swap of about 20%/yr and zero short swap.
- Requirements: beat 10,000 random-sign draws at the 99th percentile, survive doubled costs, be positive in most years and in both halves, and have a deflated Sharpe that survives N = 307 trials.

Everything can run on history before any live slot is used.
* **Red team, fatal:** The diagnostic does not test anything this account can trade. Basket predictability cannot be held under the 2-position limit. The tradeable version, lagged alt-coin returns predicting next-day ETH, is cross-asset lagged-return prediction already covered by the failed pooled ML work and the running E16 scoreboard. The paper brings no new information beyond a physics relabelling of cross-autocorrelation, whose stock-market source (nonsynchronous trading) does not exist in synchronously closed 24/7 crypto bars.
> Measures of emergence based on information theory or causal closure ask whether a group variable has a predictable law of its own, but not whether that law was produced by the members' interactions
> We introduce the interaction share S, the fraction of the forecast error of a system's no-interaction twin (the same members with every interaction removed) that the real group law eliminates

### `2026-W46-3` Global Seasonality Factor Investing

quantitative finance, [arXiv:2611.15657v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15657v1), read: full. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Per-market Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression of forward volatility-adjusted returns on the integer day within a calendar period. Models are refit yearly, with an embargo, and predictions are averaged across forecast horizons of 1 to 6 months.
* **Market analogue:** This transfers directly, with no analogy needed. The feature is the calendar day; the target is the forward 21 to 126 day vol-adjusted return on the affordable FX crosses (AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP, USDSGD).

There are four differences from the paper's setting:
- The paper traded USD-based currency futures, not crosses.
- Crypto is absent from the paper.
- Broker D1 history (2014 onward) is too short for the paper's 10-year training requirement.
- The account cannot vol-size continuously at 0.01 lots, so positions must be sign-only.
* **Claim:** Across 50 global futures, five calendar-seasonality features are fitted per market with a Gaussian kernel regression and tested walk-forward from 1995 to June 2026. The fast features (day-of-week, day-of-month) earn money before costs but lose after them. The slow features (day-of-half-year, day-of-year) stay positive after costs. An equal-weight ensemble of the seasonality models, excluding day-of-week, has near-zero correlation with carry, momentum and skew. The paper's own tables locate the edge in energy, agriculture, bonds and equities, not in currencies.
* **Hypothesis:** A slow-seasonality ensemble produces a net Sharpe on our FX crosses that beats a calendar-circular-shift null at the 99th percentile. The ensemble covers day-of-quarter, day-of-half-year and day-of-year, uses horizons of 21 to 126 days, is refit yearly, and trades the sign of the prediction. The crosses are AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP and USDSGD, net of measured spread and swap, out of sample 2005 to 2026.

The paper's own currency-sector result (ensemble alpha not significant) predicts this will FAIL. I do not expect it to pass.
* **Test:** Data and construction:
- Rebuild the five crosses daily from free FRED spot series: AUD, CAD, NZD, GBP, EUR and SGD against USD, from 1971, or 1999 for EUR.
- Spot-check against broker D1 bars for 2016 to 2026.

Model:
- Replicate the paper's kernel setup: Gaussian kernel, bandwidth 0.5 on normalized features, 40-day EMA vol, latency of 2 days, embargo of m+1, annual refit, and at least 10 years of training before going out of sample.
- Trade sign-only positions.
- Charge the measured spread, rollover-avoiding execution, and the actual swap on each side.

Null and failure criteria:
- Null: the same pipeline on randomly circularly shifted calendar labels (at least 2,000 draws), plus random-sign positions with matched turnover.
- It fails if any of these hold:
  - net Sharpe is not above the 99th percentile of the null;
  - the 2015 to 2026 half has net Sharpe at or below 0;
  - it does not survive doubled costs;
  - fewer than most years are positive;
  - the deflated Sharpe at N=308 is not significant.

The whole test runs on history at near-zero cost before any live slot. Given the paper's null currency result, it should be run only if a slot is genuinely free.
* **Red team, fatal:** The paper's own evidence shows no significant currency-sector seasonality, and its edge lives in sectors this account cannot trade. With about 21 annual cycles out of sample and a deflated-Sharpe bar at N=308, the test cannot detect any effect small enough to be consistent with the paper's null currency result. Expected information and expected value are both near zero, so it should not take a slot.
> In contrast, slower-moving seasonal patterns, such as day-of-quarter, day-of-half-year and day-of-year, produce positive gross performance and, with the exception of day-of-quarter, positive net performance.
> In fact, with the exception of the currency sector, all other sectors display statistically significant alpha values.

### `2026-W46-4` Implied directional accuracy for trading strategies with fixed holding periods

quantitative finance, [doi:10.1016/j.frl.2026.111041](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2026.111041), read: abstract. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Implied directional accuracy: the minimum DA a fixed-holding sign forecaster needs to reach a target Sharpe ratio, given return-magnitude dispersion and transaction costs. Strategy risk is P(DA < implied DA).
* **Market analogue:** Direct. The engine's pooled hourly model (E16), and any daily sign rule held one D1 bar or a fixed N bars on the 9 affordable CFDs, is a fixed-holding sign forecaster. The cost input is the measured spread, commission and the lopsided swap. Return dispersion is the distribution of absolute N-bar returns per instrument.
* **Claim:** For strategies with a fixed holding period, the probability of missing a target Sharpe ratio equals the probability that the forecast's directional accuracy (DA) falls below a threshold implied by that target. The classical 'implied precision' sets that threshold too low because it ignores the spread of return sizes and transaction costs. The paper derives an 'implied DA' that includes both. It reports that this mapping is closer to backtested Sharpe ratios and that strategy risk under it is higher at every target Sharpe. No market effect or tradeable anomaly is claimed.
* **Hypothesis:** Not a trading hypothesis; this is a screening and audit tool. Falsifiable prediction: suppose a cost- and dispersion-aware implied-DA mapping is fitted on 2014-2022 broker D1 data for ETHUSD, LTCUSD, BCHUSD, ADAUSD, AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP and USDSGD. For fixed holding periods of 1 and 20 bars, its predicted net Sharpe will track the backtested net Sharpe of the engine's already-counted sign rules (E10/E13/E15 families) with smaller absolute error than the constant-payoff, zero-cost implied-precision mapping. On 2023-2026 its required DA will exceed the realised DA of every such rule at a net Sharpe target of 0.5.
* **Test:** Run on history only; no live slot and no new trading rule, so N does not grow.
1. Take the per-trade sign series already produced by E10/E13/E15 on held broker bars.
2. For each rule, compute realised DA, mean absolute holding-period return and the measured cost per trade, including the swap by side.
3. Write down the implied-DA formula. The abstract does not give it, so either obtain the full text or derive the fixed-holding sign-forecast Sharpe from first principles before looking at results. Pre-register it.
4. Compute the analytical Sharpe at each rule's realised DA under both mappings and compare with the backtested net Sharpe.

Null: the two mappings have equal mean absolute error (paired test across rules).

The tool fails if the implied-DA error is not smaller, or if its threshold moves outside the DA sampling error when parameters are re-estimated on 2023-2026. If it passes, use it as a pre-slot gate: no candidate gets a slot unless its in-sample DA confidence band clears implied DA at the target net Sharpe.
* **Red team, fatal:** The test cannot fail in an informative way. Both predictions are true by construction. The cost-aware mapping must beat a zero-cost strawman, and every rule it is checked against has already failed. The resulting gate is a re-expression of net Sharpe, which the engine already computes and screens on more strictly.
> For strategies with fixed holding periods, the strategy risk is equivalent to the probability that the model’s DA falls below the DA threshold implied by a given target Sharpe ratio.
> this equation assumes a constant payoff and no transaction costs, thereby understating the threshold under the fixed holding setup.
