## This week in plain words (model synthesis)

**Connections across fields**

* Mathematics (W45-1) and economics (W45-3) hit the same wall: the cost of holding a position overnight. Both try to rescue a weak signal by trading it differently. W45-1 trades less often inside a no-trade band, and W45-3 sits out turbulent months. Both were killed because the cost that matters on this account is financing (the broker's overnight swap), which W45-1's cost model leaves out and which, by the red team's assessment, already makes net carry on these crosses near zero or negative. Changing when or how often you trade does not touch a cost that comes from holding the position at all.
* Physics (W45-2) and economics (W45-3) both use a slow 'state of the market' reading as an on/off switch. In W45-2 that reading is how strongly prices are being pulled back toward their average. In W45-3 it is a shock to global currency volatility. On these four FX crosses, both switches can only be attached to things that have already failed here: the physics estimator reduces to lag-1 autocorrelation and repeats the killed OU mean-reversion-speed filter, and the economics filter sits on top of carry that has failed twice. A regime filter can only help a strategy that has something to filter, and neither base strategy does.
* Mathematics (W45-1) and physics (W45-2) describe one picture from two sides: a quantity pulled back toward a target. In W45-1 the controller pulls the position back to the signal at the edge of a band. In W45-2 a restoring force pulls the price back toward its mean. Both assume a smooth, continuously adjustable quantity. The red team's objections point to the same mismatch: the engine's signals jump, and the account can only hold 0 or ±1 minimum lot. That mismatch matters more than the choice of model.

**What this should change**

* No market test ran this week, so no belief about any market should move. All three kills follow from results the engine already holds.
* Raise the importance of financing in the engine's cost model. W45-1's red team calls it the dominant cost on this account, and W45-3's red team cites the broker's lopsided swap. Before any new idea is weighed, it should be checked against overnight financing, not only against spread and commission.
* Treat the account as a choice among three states (-1, 0, +1 minimum lot), not as continuous position sizing. With about 85 dollars and a 0.01-lot minimum, any idea that depends on fine sizing, such as bands, partial rebalancing, or scaling positions by volatility, cannot be implemented as written. The real question is whether to hold one lot.
* Treat 'resilience', 'restoring rate' and 'critical slowing down' ideas from physics as the same as lag-1 autocorrelation or mean-reversion speed, unless the source supplies a specific non-stationary method beyond the abstract. On these FX crosses that family has already been killed once.
* Accept that the statistical bar is now very high. The red team estimates that about 1.5 independent bets a month over 11 years cannot clear a deflated Sharpe ratio after 307 trials. Slow monthly ideas on four crosses are underpowered almost by construction. This argues for better testing methods before more ideas.

**Gaps**

* The 14 cost-killed candidates are not identified anywhere in the engine notes. This blocks any rescue attempt and any check of which cost kills them.
* No card or queue item models overnight financing directly, even though financing is described as the dominant cost.
* Nothing in the room addresses the account's real decision: when to hold a single minimum lot versus stay flat. Most source papers assume positions can be sized continuously.
* W45-1's paper takes the tracking weight 'a' as given. Nothing available says how to set it from how fast a signal's edge decays.
* W45-2's non-stationary refinements were available only from the abstract. Without the full method, the idea cannot be told apart from lag-1 autocorrelation.
* W45-3's paper reports weaker predictability for non-USD bases. Whether its forecasting power survives on crosses, which strip out the dollar component, is untested and unknown.
* Queue item regime-trend-6 sits at priority 0.0 and nothing this week bears on it. It should be explicitly kept or dropped rather than left to linger.

**Suggested next steps**

* `2026-W42-3`: The binding problem this week was statistical power after 307 trials, not a shortage of ideas. Anytime-valid promotion tests address how to judge an adaptive research engine that keeps running tests. It is also the top-priority item in the queue (12.5).

If financing dominates, as the red team says, then execution-cost work such as queue item 2026-W44-2 (limit vs market orders) can be deprioritised with evidence rather than by assumption.

## Idea cards

### `2026-W45-1` Singular Tracking of Fractional Brownian Motion under Proportional Costs

mathematics, [arXiv:2611.01727v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01727v1), read: truncated at 120000 chars of 167837. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Singular stochastic control: causal tracking of fractional Brownian motion with quadratic tracking loss and proportional (total-variation) adjustment cost. The optimal policy in the Brownian case is reflection at a band, with an explicit band width and cell constant.
* **Market analogue:** Theta = the position a pre-cost signal would like to hold (for example a volatility-scaled continuous signal). phi = the position actually held. lambda*b = half-spread plus commission per unit traded. a = the weight placed on deviating from the signal's position, which the paper takes as given; in a market it would have to be set from how fast the signal's edge decays, and the paper says nothing about that. sigma and H = the amplitude and roughness of the target path, not of the price. What the analogy could get wrong: (1) a quadratic tracking loss is not P&L, so a cheaper tracker can still lose all of the edge it fails to capture; (2) the engine's signals are mostly sign or threshold rules that jump, not fBm-like paths; (3) at about 85 dollars and a 0.01-lot minimum, held positions are effectively {-1, 0, +1} minimum lots, so a continuous band policy cannot really be implemented. The live account mostly reduces to choosing whether to hold one lot at all.
* **Claim:** The paper proves that when a controller tracks a rough target (fractional Brownian motion with Hurst H) and pays a proportional cost on every adjustment, the best achievable total cost scales as (cost per unit)^(2H/(1+H)), with tracking error of order cost^(H/(1+H)). For a Brownian target (H = 1/2) the optimal policy keeps the error inside a fixed band [-d*, d*] with d* = (3/2)^(1/3) in normalised units, and at the optimum the cost splits 1/3 tracking error and 2/3 adjustment. Knowing the target's past does not lower the long-run constant. The paper is a statement about tracking a known target. It says nothing about returns or about whether any target is worth tracking.
* **Hypothesis:** On the 14 candidates classified as 'real edge before costs, removed by costs', recompute each as a continuous target position on its tradeable instruments (ETHUSD, ADAUSD, BCHUSD, LTCUSD, AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP, USDSGD). The candidates are not identified in the engine notes. Replace daily rebalancing with a no-trade band around the target whose half-width follows the paper's Brownian scaling, delta proportional to (cost_per_unit * sigma_target^2 / a)^(1/3). The hypothesis has two parts. (a) For at least one candidate, the band version's net Sharpe exceeds the 99th percentile of a random-sign null run through the same band and the same costs. (b) Re-optimising the band under doubled costs raises realised trading-cost drag by roughly 2^(2/3), about 1.6x, not 2x. Falsified if (a) fails for every candidate, or if the measured cost-scaling exponent at the optimum is near 1, outside about 0.5 to 0.8.
* **Test:** History only, run on Databricks before using any slot. Data: broker D1 bars (held) and measured spread, commission and swap (held). Steps: (1) Rebuild the pre-cost target series of the cost-killed candidates. (2) Fit the band width on 2015-2020 for a grid of tracking weights a, choosing a single a per candidate on that window. (3) Evaluate once on 2021-2026 at measured and at doubled costs. (4) Null: the same band applied to random-sign targets with matched turnover and the same costs, 10,000 draws. Failure means any of: the band version is not above the 99th percentile of that null; it is not positive in most years; its deflated Sharpe does not survive N = 307 (N goes up with each a tried); or cost drag at doubled costs scales nearly linearly. Before step (2), do a cheap diagnostic: check whether realised tracking cost and adjustment cost at the fitted optimum split near 1/3 to 2/3. If they do not, the Brownian-band model does not describe these targets and H must be estimated. Main risk, stated in advance: lot granularity at this account size means even a passing backtest may not be implementable live, so a pass would at most justify a forward test at a larger notional.
* **Red team, fatal:** The account cannot implement a continuous band (positions are 0 or ±1 minimum lots). The cost-killed signals already failed their holdout at exchange-futures costs, and that cheaper cost structure is a stronger rescue than a band. The paper's cost model also omits financing, which is the dominant cost here. A pass could not be traded, and a fail is already predicted by existing results.
> We study the tracking of fractional Brownian motion by adapted controls of finite variation, with quadratic loss and proportional adjustment costs.
> Reflection of $e=\psi-B$ on $[-d_{*},d_{*}]$ is optimal in the long-run cell

### `2026-W45-2` Estimating the resilience of non-stationary systems

physics, [doi:10.1038/s41467-026-78489-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78489-7), read: abstract. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Critical slowing down: the resilience (local restoring rate) of a non-stationary stochastic system, estimated as the drift coefficient of a Langevin equation via regression, with time-varying noise and uncertainty bounds.
* **Market analogue:** State variable: log price of a range-bound FX cross (AUDNZD, EURGBP, AUDCAD, EURCAD) as a deviation from a slow moving mean. Restoring rate: the speed of mean reversion of that deviation. Non-stationary forcing: drifting fair value and volatility regimes, plus intraday and rollover seasonality. Time-varying data uncertainty: time-varying volatility and spread. A tipping point would be a break out of the range into a sustained trend or a large move. Where the analogy could be wrong: markets have no fixed potential well. A falling estimated restoring rate may only be a volatility-clustering or trend-in-progress artefact rather than a precursor, and FX crosses may never sit 'near' a bifurcation in any meaningful sense. Note that a closely related idea (OU mean-reversion-speed 'small-gap regime' filter) is already killed in the queue, and trend following on these crosses already failed (3 of 5 lose before costs).
* **Claim:** From the abstract only: the paper proposes a regression-based Langevin-equation estimator of system resilience (restoring rate, i.e. critical slowing down) that handles non-stationarity, irregular sampling, data gaps and time-varying noise, gives uncertainty bounds, and is offered as a drop-in replacement for autocorrelation-based resilience estimates. It is demonstrated on Earth-system data. No market evidence is given.
* **Hypothesis:** On daily broker bars for AUDNZD, EURGBP, AUDCAD and EURCAD (2014 to 2026), fit the Langevin drift of the log-price deviation from its 60-day mean in a rolling 250-day window, with time-varying noise. When the estimated restoring rate is in its lowest quintile (lowest resilience), the next 20 trading days show a larger absolute log move than when it is in its top quintile. The estimator also predicts that move better than (a) rolling lag-1 autocorrelation and (b) trailing 20-day realised volatility. If it does not beat both baselines out of sample (2021 to 2026, after fitting choices on 2014 to 2020), the analogy adds nothing tradeable here.
* **Test:** Cheapest honest test, run entirely on held history before spending the last P1 slot. It is a prediction check, not a strategy, so it costs no live risk. Data: held D1 bars for the four crosses. Step 1: compute the rolling Langevin restoring-rate estimate (an OLS regression of the daily change in deviation on the lagged deviation, with heteroskedastic weights from trailing volatility as a stand-in for the paper's time-varying uncertainty), plus rolling AR(1) and trailing realised volatility. Step 2: measure the Spearman rank correlation between each predictor and the forward 20-day absolute log return, using non-overlapping windows. Also run a regression of the forward move on all three predictors together. Null: a block-bootstrap (20-day blocks) of the predictor series, which breaks alignment with future moves. Failure if any of these hold: the restoring-rate coefficient is not significant at the 1% level after the joint regression with realised volatility; it fails in the 2021 to 2026 holdout; or it adds nothing over AR(1). Only if it passes would a trading form be pre-registered, for example a straddle-like breakout entry in the low-resilience state. That form would face the full criteria: random-sign null at the 99th percentile, doubled costs, most years positive, and a deflated Sharpe against N = 307. Expect it to fail, because vol clustering is the obvious confound and the killed OU-speed idea covers similar ground.
* **Red team, fatal:** Two problems combine. As specifiable, the restoring-rate estimator is lag-1 autocorrelation relabelled (an OLS slope of change on lagged deviation equals phi - 1), and the paper's non-stationary refinements are unavailable from the abstract. So the test cannot add information beyond the killed OU mean-reversion-speed item. Any tradeable form reduces to breakout or trend following on FX crosses, which already failed on this instrument set.
> A wide body of work has applied the concept of critical slowing down to estimate the stability of different Earth system components.
> Here, we introduce a method to account for non-stationarity in estimating the resilience of diverse synthetic and real-world systems via a regression-based formulation of the Langevin equation.

### `2026-W45-3` Economic Fundamentals and Short-Run Exchange Rate Prediction: A Machine-Learning Perspective

economics, [doi:10.1017/s0022109026103391](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022109026103391), read: full (open-access PDF). **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Panel elastic-net and DNN forecasts of monthly log USD exchange-rate changes from 10 country characteristics, each interacted with 6 global state variables. GVOL is the monthly average of daily cross-sectional mean absolute log FX changes, taken as an AR(1) residual. The top selected term is BILL.GVOL (coefficient +0.17 on standardized predictors).
* **Market analogue:** Carry on the account's affordable FX crosses (AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP), using 3-month interbank rate differentials from held FRED data. The trade is switched off in months that follow a large positive global-FX-volatility shock. GVOL can be rebuilt from held broker D1 bars of USD pairs. The analogy could break in two ways. First, the paper's predictability is in USD-based rates, and it reports weaker results for non-USD bases. Second, building a cross removes the common dollar component, which may be where most of the forecastability lives.
* **Claim:** Monthly US-dollar exchange rates for 10 developed currencies (1995 to 2020) are predictable out of sample once fundamentals are interacted with global state variables. The strongest predictor is the bill-yield differential interacted with global FX volatility (BILL.GVOL). When global FX volatility shocks upward, uncovered interest parity reasserts itself: high-yield currencies are expected to depreciate. In calm periods carry pays. A mean-variance portfolio using these forecasts beats an optimal carry portfolio, mostly after 2008, when plain carry flatlined.
* **Hypothesis:** On AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD and EURGBP, the rule holds the carry direction (sign of the 3-month rate differential) for one month. It goes flat in any month after GVOL's AR(1) residual lands in its expanding top quartile. Net of measured spread and swap, 2015 to 2026, this volatility-conditioned carry earns a pooled monthly return above the 99th percentile of a random-sign null. It also beats unconditioned carry on the same pairs. The 2020-10 to 2026 segment, which postdates the paper's sample, must be positive on its own.
* **Test:** Data: broker D1 bars at 21:00 UTC month-ends for the 4 crosses. Broker D1 bars for the paper's USD majors are used to build GVOL (mean absolute daily log change across pairs, monthly average, then an AR(1) residual fitted on an expanding window). FRED 3-month interbank rates are held. Costs: measured spread plus actual broker swap for each side over the holding month. Rebalance at month-end, avoiding the 21:00 rollover by entering at the next liquid hour. Fix the rule entirely in advance: no fitted coefficients, quartile threshold on an expanding window. Null 1: 10,000 random-sign draws with the same holding pattern and costs. Null 2: unconditioned carry on the same pairs, which the engine already found a wash. Failure is any of the following: below the 99th-percentile random-sign result; no improvement over unconditioned carry; does not survive doubled costs; fewer than most years positive; or a negative 2020-10 to 2026 segment. Power caveat: roughly 140 months by 4 pairs, with the flat filter removing about a quarter, gives a deflated Sharpe at N=307 that will be hard to clear. Treat a pass as forward-test-worthy only. This runs fully on history before any live slot, but P1 has 1 slot left.
* **Red team, fatal:** The underlying carry on these crosses has already failed twice on this broker (pre-programme, and E10 from 2015 to 2026 net of financing). A volatility filter that removes about a quarter of months cannot create an edge where the net carry leg is near zero or negative after the broker's lopsided swap. On top of that, roughly 1.5 effective independent bets a month over 11 years cannot clear a deflated Sharpe at N=307. The last P1 slot would be spent on a test that cannot pass.
> We show that fundamentals do matter, but their effects vary with global financial conditions.
> In terms of magnitude, the most important predictor inFigure 2 is BILL.GVOL, with a coefficient estimate of 0.17.
