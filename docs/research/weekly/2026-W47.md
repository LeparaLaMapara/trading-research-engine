## This week in plain words (model synthesis)

**Connections across fields**

* Cards 2026-W47-1, 2026-W47-4 and 2026-W47-5 are three separate optimisation papers that share one idea: when trading costs money, trade less. Card 1 plans many periods ahead but acts one step at a time. Card 4 leaves a no-trade zone around the target position. Card 5 moves only part of the way toward the target and shrinks noisy estimates. On this account all three reduce to the same thing. With positions of either 0 or 0.01 lots and at most two open at once, each method becomes a single 'trade or don't' threshold. None of them can add value without a signal that makes money before costs.
* Mathematics card 2026-W47-2 and finance card 2026-W47-3 bear on the same weak assumption. Card 2's monitoring method assumes the P&L series is statistically stable over time, and that volatility clustering behaves like 'weak dependence'. Card 3 describes volatility in crypto that rises and falls with the exchange-holiday calendar. If card 3's effect is real, the daily crypto series that card 2 would monitor is not stable over time. Card 2's own caveat that crypto may lack finite moments above order 4 points the same way.
* Mathematics card 2026-W47-2 and queued finance item 2026-W42-3 ask the same question: when can a forward test be stopped and the rule promoted or halted, without the statistics being invalidated by repeated looks? Card 2's red team says they are duplicates. Card 2 adds a specific variance construction (self-normalization).
* Economics card 2026-W47-6 and finance card 2026-W47-7 both rank assets by the shape of their recent price history rather than its average. Card 6 scores how a loss-averse investor (prospect theory) would value the last 60 months of returns. Card 7 measures how straight-line the last 11 months of prices were (trend R²). Both bet that the way past returns arrived predicts future returns. Card 7 was killed because it sits inside the momentum family, which has already failed here (E10, A01). Card 6 survives because its bet is relative value between the two currencies of each cross, not trend-following.

**What this should change**

* Portfolio-construction and execution methods cannot create an edge on this account. Cards 1, 4 and 5 were all killed for the same reason: no signal survives costs on the affordable instruments (E07 and E08 demoted by A01, E09 to E15 failed). Papers of this kind should rank low until a signal survives.
* For holds of a week or longer, the overnight financing charge (swap) is the dominant cost. A no-trade band makes it worse by keeping positions open longer (card 4's red team). The engine should treat 'trade less' as a remedy for spread costs only, not swap costs.
* Trend and momentum on the nine affordable instruments stays closed. Adding a trend-quality filter (card 7) does not change this. The paper's own currency result weakens in the strong-dollar period that covers the whole test window, and the tradable cross-section is too small to pass a deflated Sharpe at N=307.
* Anytime-valid gating on daily P&L alone is too slow to be useful here. Card 2's red team estimates roughly a decade of forward data before anything could be promoted. Its constant-mean assumption would also miss the kind of edge decay A01 found (E07's edge concentrated in 2018-2022). The gate becomes decision-relevant only if it runs on pooled, higher-frequency outcomes such as E16's hourly pooled results.
* Lower crypto volatility on joint European and Asian exchange holidays (card 3) may be true, but it is not actionable. A CFD account cannot sell volatility, no live rule exists whose stops or sizing it could condition, and the data overlaps the paper's own sample. It should not be revisited unless a live crypto rule needs volatility-based sizing.
* Card 2026-W47-6 (prospect theory on currencies) is the only new hypothesis this week to survive red team (information 3, value 2, cost 2). It is the engine's only fresh candidate source of return, and it has a clean out-of-sample window after the paper's February 2018 end.

**Gaps**

* Three of seven cards were portfolio-construction papers with nothing to test. The intake is not finding new sources of return. Card 6 was the only one.
* Card 6's tradable version holds AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD and EURGBP at once, but the account caps open positions at two. The card does not say how to choose or rotate the two.
* Nothing in the cards or the queue directly tackles detecting a fading edge in a live or forward-tested rule. Card 2 tracks a constant mean, and regime-trend-6 (drawdown control) sits at priority 0.
* It has not been checked whether crypto daily P&L on the broker's 21:00 UTC bars has the finite moments card 2's method requires, or how much the 21:00 bar timing shifts calendar effects such as card 3's.
* Swap is repeatedly named as the deciding cost for longer holds (cards 4 and 7), but no card or queue item measures or models it directly. That includes the large asymmetry where crypto longs pay about 20% a year and shorts pay nothing.
* The top-priority queued item 2026-W40-1 (auditing explainable-AI trading policies) connects to nothing this week. Its priority of 12.5 may be stale.

### `2026-W47-1` Multi-Period Trading via Convex Optimization

foundations, [doi:10.1561/9781680833294](https://doi.org/10.1561/9781680833294), read: truncated at 120000 chars of 126722. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Cost-aware portfolio optimisation and receding-horizon control: maximise forecast return minus scaled transaction cost, holding cost and risk, executing only the first planned trade.
* **Market analogue:** In principle it could size and smooth positions on the engine's affordable CFDs, with swap as the holding cost and spread as the linear transaction-cost term. But it needs a return forecast with net edge, and the engine has none: A01 demoted E07 and E08, and E09 to E15 failed. The engine's real constraints (0.01-lot minimum, at most 2 positions, integer lots) are exactly the nonconvex constraints the paper says to avoid or handle heuristically.
* **Claim:** This is a tutorial on methodology, not a market effect. It sets out a model for simulating multi-period trading. Single-period and multi-period (model predictive control) convex optimisation then turns given forecasts into trades, trading expected return against risk, transaction cost and holding cost. It deliberately supplies no forecasts. Its numerical examples use synthetic forecasts built from realised returns, so it states no effect size that survives realistic costs.
* **Hypothesis:** Nothing testable here. The paper offers no return predictor and no market effect. Its examples use forecasts built from realised returns, and it says its numbers should not be taken seriously. A cost-aware execution layer only adds value on top of a signal with edge before costs. No such signal currently survives on this engine's instruments, so no falsifiable trading hypothesis follows from this text alone.
* **Test:** None now; do not spend a slot. If a future signal passes pre-registration with net edge, the cheap reuse is a historical check: compare that signal's raw positions against the same signal passed through a cost-penalised rebalancing step. Use broker D1 bars with measured spread and swap. The null is no improvement in net Sharpe, and failure is no gain at doubled costs. The trade-aversion multiplier must be fixed in advance, not grid-searched: the paper's 410-point and 390-point hyper-parameter searches would add to N=307.
* **Red team, fatal:** There is no hypothesis to falsify. The paper deliberately supplies no forecast, the engine has no signal with net edge to feed it, and the account's 0.01-lot, two-position, integer constraints reduce the optimiser to a binary trade-or-don't threshold. A test would teach nothing and would spend a slot.
> In this paper we do not address a critical component in a trading algorithm, the predictions or forecasts of future quantities.
> Of course this is not a real return forecast, since it uses the actual realized return; but our purpose here is only to illustrate the ideas and methods.

### `2026-W47-2` Anytime-valid inference for time series via self-normalization

mathematics, [arXiv:2611.26536v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26536v1), read: full. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Asymptotic confidence sequences via self-normalization (forward and three-block normalizers) under geometric physical dependence, finite moments above order 4 and strict stationarity.
* **Market analogue:** X_t is the daily net P&L of a candidate rule on a broker instrument (21:00 UTC D1 bars). Theta is either its mean daily return (edge) or a lower quantile or expected shortfall of daily P&L (risk). Burn-in m is the number of days of the forward test before the first look. The stopping rule is 'promote when the interval excludes zero' or 'halt when the loss quantile exceeds the risk budget'. What the analogy assumes and could get wrong: (1) Edge is a constant theta. A01 showed E07's edge was concentrated in 2018-2022, so the sequence tracks a stale full-sample mean and will not flag decay. (2) Finite moments above order 4. Crypto daily returns may not satisfy this. (3) Volatility clustering behaves like geometric weak dependence.
* **Claim:** The paper builds self-normalized, anytime-valid confidence sequences for parameters of stationary, weakly dependent time series, including the mean, quantiles, expectiles, expected shortfall and the lag-one coefficient. Their simultaneous coverage over all monitoring times converges to 1-alpha as the burn-in m grows, without estimating the long-run variance. It is a monitoring and stopping tool. It contains no market mechanism and no source of trading edge.
* **Hypothesis:** There is no tradeable market hypothesis in this paper. What can be tested is whether the tool can serve as a monitoring gate for E16 and future forward tests. Applied with three-block, kappa=1/4, LIL weight, m=250 days and nominal 95%, the sequence for the mean daily return of random-sign, 20-day-persistent strategies on real broker D1 returns should keep its zero-crossing false-alarm rate at or below 7% over the full 2015-2026 history. The instruments are ETHUSD, ADAUSD, LTCUSD, BCHUSD, USDSGD, EURGBP, AUDCAD, AUDNZD and EURCAD. The hypothesis fails if the false-alarm rate exceeds 8% on any crypto instrument, or on the pooled FX set.
* **Test:** Data: held broker D1 closes for the nine affordable instruments. For each instrument, generate 2,000 strategies whose sign is drawn at random and held for 20-day blocks. This is a true null with E[s*r]=0, and it keeps the persistence and volatility clustering of a trend rule. Simulate the Brownian critical value for three-block (kappa=1/4, LIL weight) once with 20,000 paths, as the paper does. Run the sequence from day 250 to the end of the data and record the fraction of null paths whose interval ever excludes zero. Repeat with the forward normalizer and m=500. Null: the false-alarm rate is about 5%. Failure: more than 8% on any crypto instrument or on the pooled FX set. That would show heavy tails or regime shifts break the calibration, and the tool must not gate promotions. Descriptive add-on, not a pass criterion: run it on the reconstructed E07 net series and report whether and when it would have excluded zero, and whether it ever reversed after September 2022. The point is to show its blindness to decay. This runs entirely on history on Databricks and uses no slot or live capital. It is a methodology check, so it should not add to N=307 as a strategy trial. It overlaps with the queued anytime-valid promotion-test item and should be merged with it.
* **Red team, fatal:** Duplicate of the queued anytime-valid promotion-test item. This is the same gating question with a different variance construction, and the card itself asks to be merged. On daily P&L the method also needs roughly a decade of forward data to promote anything, so as a standalone card it cannot change a decision for this account. Hand the self-normalized construction, and a time-to-decision power calculation for daily versus hourly pooled (E16) outcomes, to the queued item as inputs.
> A key advantage is that the construction avoids direct estimation of the long-run variance, which can be particularly challenging for nonlinear parameters under serial dependence.
> Conventional confidence intervals are designed for a fixed, predetermined sample size and generally do not preserve their coverage guarantees under repeated peeking or data-dependent stopping.

### `2026-W47-3` Equity-market closures and cryptocurrency volatility

quantitative finance, [doi:10.1016/j.iref.2026.106070](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iref.2026.106070), read: abstract. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Calendar-driven volatility compression: an exogenous closure of the venues that carry information flow reduces realised volatility in an asset that keeps trading.
* **Market analogue:** Directly market-based. The asset is the engine's crypto CFDs (ETHUSD, BCHUSD, LTCUSD, ADAUSD). The treatment is joint Europe plus Asia-Pacific exchange holidays on weekdays. The outcome is |D1 return|. Caveat: the broker's D1 bars run 21:00 to 21:00 UTC, which may not line up with the paper's daily bars or with Asian closure days. The effect may be diluted or shifted by a day.
* **Claim:** On weekdays when European and Asia-Pacific equity exchanges are closed at the same time, crypto daily absolute returns are lower. The equal-weighted index of 13 coins is about 0.96 percentage points lower, about 30% of its mean daily move. Bitcoin's absolute return is lower by about a quarter of its mean. The paper reports 66 such dates from 2015 to 2026. No compression is found on Asia-only closures. The claim is about the size of moves (volatility), not their direction.
* **Hypothesis:** On weekdays when major European and Asia-Pacific exchanges are both closed, |D1 return| of the broker's ETHUSD (and LTCUSD, BCHUSD, ADAUSD) bars is lower than on other weekdays of the same month and year. The prediction is a mean reduction of at least 20% of the instrument's mean |D1 return|, estimated with month-year fixed effects. This is a volatility fact, not a directional edge. On its own it gives no trade the engine can place, because a CFD account cannot sell volatility. At best it would condition stop or size choices in a future rule.
* **Test:** Cheapest test, run on history only, with no live slot. Data: broker D1 bars for ETHUSD, LTCUSD, BCHUSD and ADAUSD, held from about 2017. Exchange holiday calendars for the main EU and APAC venues are free, for example the exchange_calendars package. Build the joint-closure weekday dummy, then regress |r_t| on it with month-year fixed effects. Run the test twice: once on 21:00 UTC broker bars and once on bars re-aggregated to the UTC calendar day from held ticks or H1 data. Null: closure-date mean |r| equals the mean of randomly drawn non-closure weekdays from the same year. Use 10,000 permutations and count the result as passing only beyond the 99th percentile. Failure: no reduction of at least 15% of mean |r| on ETHUSD, or a reduction present only in pre-2022 years. Even a pass only licenses a conditioning variable for a later pre-registered rule. With about 6 events a year, it is as underpowered as E12 for any standalone trade. It must also be counted toward N=307.
* **Red team, fatal:** No path to P&L or cost reduction on this account. It is a volatility-size fact with no direction. CFDs cannot sell volatility, and there is no live rule whose stops or sizing it could condition. The available data also lies inside the paper's own sample, so even a pass would be a re-measurement, not new evidence.
> On these dates an equal-weighted index of absolute daily returns is about 0.96 percentage points lower, some 30 percent of its mean daily move, and Bitcoin's is lower by about a quarter of its own mean.
> The calendar contains 66 such dates, 65 of which enter the primary regressions.

### `2026-W47-4` Multiperiod portfolio optimization with multiple risky assets and general transaction costs

foundations, [doi:10.1016/j.jbankfin.2016.04.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2016.04.002), read: abstract. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** A no-trade (hysteresis) region in multiperiod mean-variance optimisation under proportional transaction costs, with trading to the band boundary rather than to the frictionless target.
* **Market analogue:** Asset weights correspond to CFD position sizes. The proportional cost is the measured spread plus commission plus daily swap. The frictionless target is a signal's desired position. At 85 USD with a 0.01-lot minimum and at most 2 positions, sizes are effectively 0 or 0.01 lots. The multi-asset parallelogram therefore collapses into a one-dimensional entry/exit threshold on each signal, which is hysteresis rather than continuous rebalancing. The analogy assumes there is a pre-cost edge to protect. That could be wrong: E10 found best t 2.70 before banding, and most families were OUT or UNDECIDED.
* **Claim:** The paper makes no market-return claim. It is a portfolio-control result: with proportional transaction costs, a multiperiod mean-variance investor should not trade inside a no-trade region, and the authors give a closed-form parallelogram for that region. With market-impact costs, the investor should trade only to the boundary of a rebalancing region that depends on the current state. The abstract says that ignoring costs and acting myopically 'may be large' in losses, but gives no numbers, assets or periods.
* **Hypothesis:** Take one pre-registered signal family from E10: time-series momentum on the affordable instruments ETHUSD, ADAUSD, BCHUSD, LTCUSD, AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP and USDSGD. Add one pre-fixed hysteresis band: enter when the signal crosses +k, exit only when it crosses back past -k, with k fixed in advance at the value that halves turnover on 2015-2020. The banded version will have a higher net-of-cost Sharpe than the unbanded version on 2021-2026. The hypothesis is falsified if the improvement is not positive, or if the banded rule fails the engine's own pass criteria: beat the 99th percentile of a random-sign null with the same costs, survive doubled costs, be positive in most years, and keep a deflated Sharpe positive at N=308.
* **Test:** History only; no live slot. Use broker D1 bars (21:00 UTC) for the 9 affordable instruments with measured spread and swap, including crypto longs paying about 20% a year and shorts paying nothing. Set the band width on 2015-2020 using turnover alone, not P&L. Then run once on 2021-2026. Null 1: the unbanded rule has the same net Sharpe. Null 2: random-sign positions with the same band and the same turnover profile, at the 99th percentile. Any of these counts as failure: a net Sharpe gain of 0 or less; t below 3.0 for the banded rule; loss under doubled costs; a negative deflated Sharpe at N=308. P1 has no slots left, so this needs a slot in the next programme. Expect failure: banding cannot create the pre-cost forecast strength that E10 did not find.
* **Red team, fatal:** The proposed signal family (time-series momentum / trend on the 9 affordable instruments) has no surviving pre-cost edge in the out-of-sample window: E08, E10 and A01, plus the pre-programme trend results. For week-long holds the dominant cost is swap, which a hysteresis band increases rather than reduces. The test cannot pass and teaches nothing we do not already know.
> For proportional transaction costs, we give a closed-form expression for a no-trade region, shaped as a multi-dimensional parallelogram
> For market impact costs, we show that at each period it is optimal to trade to the boundary of a state-dependent rebalancing region.

### `2026-W47-5` Parameter Uncertainty in Multiperiod Portfolio Optimization with Transaction Costs

foundations, [doi:10.1017/s002210901500054x](https://doi.org/10.1017/s002210901500054x), read: full (open-access PDF). **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Garleanu-Pedersen partial adjustment towards a mean-variance target under quadratic market-impact costs, combined with Kan-Zhou shrinkage of plug-in portfolio weights.
* **Market analogue:** The target corresponds to a multi-asset CFD book weighted by estimated mean over covariance. The trading rate beta corresponds to how far the book is rebalanced each day. Lambda corresponds to market impact, which does not exist at 0.01 lots: this engine's costs are proportional (spread) plus asymmetric swap, not quadratic. The paper's edge input mu is the sample mean drift, and the engine has no instrument with a demonstrated positive drift net of swap. The analogy would predict gains from smoothing and shrinkage. That is wrong here: 1. there is no positive mu to shrink towards; 2. minimum lot size makes partial adjustment impossible; 3. at most 2 positions rules out a 15-50 asset portfolio.
* **Claim:** For a multiperiod mean-variance investor facing quadratic (market-impact) transaction costs and iid normal returns, the expected utility loss from estimating parameters equals the single-period Kan-Zhou loss times a multiperiod factor. Shrinking the sample Markowitz portfolio towards the minimum-variance portfolio, and optionally slowing the trading rate, improves out-of-sample net Sharpe. Evidence is a 15-commodity-futures panel (2004-2012) and simulated data with N=25 and 50.
* **Hypothesis:** Nothing testable here for this engine. The paper is a portfolio-construction method, not a source of return. Its gains require a positive expected-return vector, many assets, fractional rebalancing and quadratic impact costs. This engine has none of these: there is no surviving edge, positions come in 0.01-lot steps, at most 2 positions are allowed, and costs are spread plus swap. The one transferable insight, that high costs should make you trade less aggressively, is already embodied in the engine's finding that costs are the binding constraint. It does not create an edge where E10's 431-trial screen found none.
* **Test:** No test recommended; spending a slot would add to N=307 without a plausible source of edge. If ever revisited, the only honest check would need several instruments with a pre-registered positive net drift. It would compare a fixed-fraction partial-adjustment rule against an all-at-once switch to the same target on D1 bars, with doubled costs and a random-sign null. That cannot run until such a target exists.
* **Red team, fatal:** No positive expected-return input exists on this engine, and the mechanism needs a many-asset, fractionally rebalanced book under quadratic impact costs. The account has none of these (2 positions max, 0.01-lot steps, proportional spread plus swap costs), so there is nothing to test.
> First, quadratic transaction costs are appropriate to model market impact costs, which arise when the investor makes large trades that distort market prices.
> Hence, larger trading costs or higher impatience factor make the investor trade less aggressively, and this offsets the uncertainty of the inputs

### `2026-W47-6` Prospect Theory and Currency Returns: Empirical Evidence

economics, [doi:10.1017/s0022109026103469](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022109026103469), read: truncated at 120000 chars of 135159. **priority 3.0**

* **Concept:** Cumulative prospect theory value (Tversky-Kahneman 1992 parameters) of an asset's trailing return distribution, used as a cross-sectional mispricing signal.
* **Market analogue:** Already a market paper. The engine cannot trade the 15-currency quintile portfolio. The nearest version is pairwise: on each affordable cross (AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP), compare the USD-based prospect-theory values of the two legs and go long the leg with the lower value. The cross's spot return is roughly the base leg's USD return minus the quote leg's.
* **Claim:** On 15 developed-market currencies against the USD (1990 to Feb 2018), a prospect-theory value computed from each currency's last 60 monthly spot returns predicts the next month's excess return with a negative sign. Buying the lowest-value quintile and selling the highest earns about 5% a year before costs (Sharpe 0.59 a year). The spread comes mainly from spot moves, not from interest differentials.
* **Hypothesis:** Gate (out of sample, after the paper's Feb 2018 end): from Mar 2019 to Aug 2026, the monthly low-minus-high prospect-theory spot-return spread across the freely available G10 currencies against the USD is positive. Paper construction: K=60 months, alpha=0.88, lambda=2.25, gamma=0.61, delta=0.69. Tradable version: each month, hold AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD and EURGBP in the direction of the leg with lower prospect-theory value, at 0.01 lots, entered away from the 21:00 UTC rollover. Prediction: positive net return, beating 99% of random-sign monthly allocations under the same costs, and surviving doubled costs.
* **Test:** Pre-register as a history-only gate, counted in N (308). Data: FRED H.10 daily USD rates for AUD, NZD, CAD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CHF, SEK and NOK (DKK dropped as a peg), sampled on the last business day of each month from 2013-03. Signal: tk per the paper's eq. (1), K=60 monthly spot log returns, alpha=0.88, lambda=2.25, gamma=0.61, delta=0.69, with no retuning. Portfolio: each month-end, long the 3 lowest-tk currencies and short the 3 highest, equal weight, scored on next-month USD spot return. Out-of-sample window: 2018-03 to 2026-08, fixed before running. Sanity check, which does not count toward the decision: the sign over 2013-03 to 2018-02 should be positive. PASS requires all four of the following: (a) the out-of-sample mean spread is above 0; (b) it exceeds the 95th percentile of 10,000 random assignments of currencies to the long and short legs; (c) the mean is at least 0.15% a month; (d) the tk spread keeps a positive mean with t ≥ 1.5 after regressing it on two controls built the same way, using data available at each month-end: a carry tercile spread (FRED 3-month rates, on the 7 currencies that have them) and a 60-month cumulative-return reversal spread. Any failure kills the card with no further work. Step 2 runs only if all four pass. Apply the pairwise rule to AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD and EURGBP on broker D1 bars from 2018-03 onward. Use FRED-based tk ranks at month-end and enter the next broker day between 08:00 and 16:00 UTC. Charge measured spread plus actual swap. Require all of: beating the 99th percentile of a random-sign null under the same costs; surviving doubled costs; a majority of years positive; a deflated Sharpe at the running N. If everything passes except the deflated Sharpe, the rule goes only to the E16-style forward scoreboard, never to live money. Stated prior: most likely outcome is FAIL at step 1.
> A currency-level measure of prospect-theory value, constructed from the historical distribution of exchange rate returns, negatively forecasts future currency excess returns.
> produces a statistically significant return spread of 0.42% per month (5.04% per annum) and a Sharpe ratio of 0.17 per month (0.59 per annum).

### `2026-W47-7` Trended Momentum

quantitative finance, [doi:10.1017/s0022109026103275](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0022109026103275), read: truncated at 120000 chars of 159125. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** A cross-sectional double sort: first on 11-month past return (skipping month t), then within each past-return group on the R² of a linear time trend fitted to daily prices over the same window. Holding period is t+1 to t+6.
* **Market analogue:** Each broker CFD's D1 close series (21:00 UTC bars) plays the role of a stock's price path. PRET is the 11-month log return skipping the latest month. TC is the R² of daily close on a day index over that window. The cross-section is the broker's pooled FX and crypto instruments. The paper's spot-currency panel excluded carry. Here the analogue must pay the broker's swap, which the paper never faced: crypto longs pay about 20% a year, crypto shorts pay nothing, and FX swaps are small and asymmetric.
* **Claim:** Momentum is stronger when the formation-period price path is smooth. Trend clarity (TC) is the R² of a regression of daily price on time over months t-11 to t-1. In US equities, high-TC momentum earns 12.68% a year against 6.90% for conventional momentum. In a 20-currency spot panel (1980-2023, carry excluded), winner-minus-loser is significant only in the high-TC tercile. Commodities show the same direction without a significant high-minus-low gap, and government bonds show no effect. The paper reports no transaction costs anywhere.
* **Hypothesis:** Pooled panel: the broker's instruments with D1 history (FX crosses and majors plus crypto), 2015 to 2026. Each month, sort by sign of PRET (t-11 to t-1) and by TC tercile, then hold for 6 months. (a) Momentum positions (long positive PRET, short negative PRET) in the high-TC tercile earn a positive mean return net of measured spread, commission and swap. (b) That return beats the low-TC tercile's momentum return, with the high-minus-low spread significant. Restricted to the tradable nine (ETHUSD, ADAUSD, BCHUSD, LTCUSD, AUDCAD, AUDNZD, EURCAD, EURGBP, USDSGD), the high-TC filter should improve on unfiltered 12-1 time-series momentum. Falsified if high-TC net returns do not beat the random-sign null at the 99th percentile, if the high-minus-low TC gap is not positive, or if the deflated Sharpe does not survive N=307 trials.
* **Test:** Run entirely on held history before any live slot. Data: broker D1 bars for the roughly 60 instruments with enough history, measured spreads and swap rates. Construction: at each month end compute PRET and TC, with at least 200 daily observations for TC to match the paper. Form overlapping 6-month positions (equal-risk, volatility-scaled) for high, mid and low TC terciles. Score both the time-series sign version and the cross-sectional winner-minus-loser version.
