### `2026-W43-1` A Bayesian Hidden Markov Model with Singh--Maddala Emission Distributions for Heavy-Tailed Regime-Switching Time Series

statistics, [arXiv:2610.22656v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22656v1), read: full. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Hidden Markov Model (HMM) with heavy-tailed emission distributions for regime-switching time series.
* **Market analogue:** Financial asset returns (specifically daily log returns) as a heavy-tailed, regime-switching time series. Regimes correspond to distinct market states (e.g., bull/bear, high/low volatility, high/low expected return). The conditional mean of emissions corresponds to the expected daily log return in that regime. The tail index corresponds to the fatness of the tail of returns in that regime. A scale-driven extreme in returns would be predictable from the identified regime, while a tail-driven extreme would not.
* **Claim:** The HMMSM model provides superior predictive performance and identifies distinct, persistent regimes with quantifiable statistical properties (like conditional means and tail indices) in heavy-tailed, regime-switching time series, including financial asset returns.
* **Hypothesis:** A Bayesian Hidden Markov Model with Singh–Maddala emission distributions (HMMSM), applied to daily log returns of ETHUSD, will identify at least two distinct market regimes. If one regime is characterized by a statistically higher positive conditional mean daily log return (a "bullish" regime) and another by a statistically lower or negative conditional mean daily log return (a "bearish" regime), then a strategy that goes long ETHUSD CFD when the model assigns a high posterior probability to the "bullish" regime, and short when it assigns a high posterior probability to the "bearish" regime, will yield a positive Sharpe ratio after accounting for the engine's trading costs.
* **Test:** Data: Daily close-to-close log returns for ETHUSD from the engine's held broker bars (back to 2014). Method: Implement the HMMSM (using the provided open-source code) to infer regimes and their parameters (especially conditional mean returns) for ETHUSD daily log returns. Use Bayesian model selection criteria (WAIC, DIC, LOO) to determine the optimal number of regimes (K). For each day, calculate the posterior probability of being in each regime. Strategy: Define "high posterior probability" (e.g., > 0.8). If the model identifies a "bullish" regime (highest positive conditional mean log return) and a "bearish" regime (lowest or negative conditional mean log return), enter a long position when the "bullish" regime probability is high and exit when it drops or the "bearish" regime probability becomes high. Conversely, enter a short position when the "bearish" regime probability is high and exit when it drops or the "bullish" regime probability becomes high. Hold positions until regime probabilities shift. Null hypothesis: The Sharpe ratio of this strategy, after accounting for measured trading costs (spread, swap), is not significantly different from zero, or not statistically distinguishable from a random-sign strategy with equivalent costs and exposure. Failure criteria: The strategy's Sharpe ratio is less than or equal to zero, or its 99th percentile bootstrap resample (with block bootstrap to preserve autocorrelation) falls at or below zero. This test can be run on historical data first.
* **Red team, fatal:** Disguised variant of a killed idea; prohibitive costs for long positions.
> Heavy-tailed time series that exhibit regime switching are common in environmental, financial, and engineering applications, yet their statistical modeling remains challenging because conventional emission distributions — such as the Gaussian, Gamma, or Weibull — fail to capture simultaneously the extreme right-skewness, the slowly decaying upper tails, and the temporal dependence that characterize such processes.
> This paper develops a fully Bayesian Hidden Markov Model with Singh–Maddala (Burr Type XII) emission distributions (HMMSM).

### `2026-W43-2` A Model-Free Continuous-Time RL Algorithm from Discrete-Time Off-Policy Data with End-to-End Guarantees

mathematics, [arXiv:2610.28915v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28915v1), read: truncated at 120000 chars of 146239. **KILLED**

* **Concept:** Model-Free Continuous-Time Reinforcement Learning (PhiBE-PI)
* **Market analogue:** The market is modeled as a system evolving in continuous time (state $s_t$, action $a_t$, reward rate $r(s_t, a_t)$), but only discrete-time observations (OHLCV bars) are available. The state $s$ corresponds to market features (e.g., log price, log volume, log returns). The action $a$ corresponds to trading decisions (e.g., long, short, cash). The reward $r$ is the instantaneous profit/loss rate. The sampling interval $\Delta t$ is the bar duration (e.g., 4-hours). The fixed off-policy batch $\mathcal{D}$ is historical market data. The policy $\pi(s)$ is a trading strategy mapping market state to action. The Q-function $Q(s,a)$ is the expected discounted future reward from taking action $a$ in state $s$ and then following policy $\pi$. The underlying stochastic differential equation for market prices is unknown.
* **Claim:** A Model-Free Continuous-Time RL Algorithm from Discrete-Time Off-Policy Data with End-to-End Guarantees. We develop a model-free PhiBE policy iteration (PhiBE-PI) framework that uses a single state-action critic while preserving the continuous-time action-ranking scale. We further develop an implementation under linear function approximation and establish a local end-to-end suboptimality guarantee for the learned policy.
* **Hypothesis:** For ETHUSD CFD, a PhiBE-PI trading policy, using linear function approximation with `log(close_price)`, `log(volume)`, and `log(close_price / previous_close_price)` as state features and discrete actions {long 0.01 lot, short 0.01 lot, cash}, when trained and deployed on 4-hour bars, will achieve a higher Sharpe ratio after trading costs than a baseline discrete-time Q-learning policy (BE-PI) using the same features and actions, also trained and deployed on 4-hour bars.
* **Test:** 1. Implement PhiBE-PI and a standard discrete-time Q-learning (BE-PI) algorithm, both using linear function approximation and hard-greedy policy improvement. 2. Define state features as `log(close_price)`, `log(volume)`, and `log(close_price / previous_close_price)` from 4-hour bars. Actions are {long 0.01 lot, short 0.01 lot, cash}. The reward for a 4-hour period is the P&L after accounting for spread and overnight swap costs. 3. Train both algorithms on a fixed off-policy batch of historical ETHUSD CFD 4-hour bar data. 4. Simulate trading performance for both policies over a multi-year historical period, applying measured trading costs (spread, swap). 5. Null hypothesis: The Sharpe ratio of the PhiBE-PI policy is not statistically significantly higher than that of the BE-PI policy (e.g., p-value > 0.01 in a bootstrap test for difference in Sharpe ratios). 6. Failure criteria: If the PhiBE-PI policy's Sharpe ratio is not higher than the BE-PI policy's, or if it fails to beat a random-sign null at the 99th percentile with the same costs, or if it does not survive doubled costs, or if most years are not positive. This test can be run entirely on historical data.
* **Red team, fatal:** The binding constraint is the cost of trading, not prediction. High costs (especially for crypto longs) have consistently killed similar ideas, and there's no evidence this particular ML approach would overcome that. The paper's theoretical benefits are not shown to translate to real-world financial profitability after costs, and prior ML attempts on this instrument have failed.
