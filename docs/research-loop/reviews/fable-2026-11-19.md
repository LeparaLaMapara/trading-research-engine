# Strategy advice for the SA trading research engine (2026-11-19)

## Verdict first

Nothing here makes meaningful money at this size. Expected rand per year is capital x volatility x Sharpe.

| Pot | Realistic outcome |
|---|---|
| cTrader, USD 79 | About USD 15 a year even at Sharpe 1 and 20% vol. One 0.01 lot of EURUSD is about 14x the account, so vol targeting is impossible. This is an execution lab, not a profit centre. |
| EasyEquities, R50,000 | Cash near the 7.25% repo rate (September 2026; a decision is due today) pays roughly R3,500 risk free. A real 2% edge adds R1,000. |
| Savings | R1,000 a month added is R12,000 a year, which beats every strategy below. |

Order of operations: wrapper and costs, then cheap beta, then one slow rule, then research that builds a verifiable record for when capital is larger.

## 1. Ranked families

**1. Slow asset-class trend on JSE ETFs, inside the TFSA (EasyEquities, monthly by hand)**
- **Evidence:** Faber's 10-month moving-average rule; [Moskowitz/Ooi/Pedersen](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets/Time-Series-Momentum-Original-Paper-Data) across 58 futures; [Hurst/Ooi/Pedersen](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/hurst.pdf) back to 1880. [Faber's own out-of-sample record](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist) cut the 2008 loss, then lagged in six of the next eight years.
- **Expected Sharpe:** 0.3 to 0.5, nearly all beta. The gain is a smaller drawdown, not extra return.
- **Costs:** ETF purchases are [exempt from STT](https://www.jse.co.za/node/1099) and brokerage is about 0.25% a side. A handful of switches a year costs roughly R200 to R400.
- **Small-player edge:** no career risk from lagging a benchmark, and tax-free rotation.
- **Data:** free (Yahoo .JO ETF prices; check dividend adjustment).
- **Test:** one trial, the fixed 10-month rule on five asset-class ETFs (SA equity, global equity, bonds, property, gold) against equal-weight buy and hold. The window starts at the latest ETF inception and the last 40% is the vault. Pass if vault max drawdown is at most 0.7x buy and hold and Sharpe is no more than 0.1 lower, net of 0.4% per switch.

**2. JSE equity momentum, via the ETF rather than stock picking (EasyEquities, hold for years)**
- **Evidence:** [Muller and Ward 2013](https://repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/21852) find significant style effects for 1985 to 2011, and a later thesis using their method calls momentum their best performer. A [Wits thesis](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/items/6dcc0b5b-f47c-4570-9835-176686840919) finds it profitable at 3 to 12 months. I could not read Muller and Ward's return figures, so I cite direction only.
- **Haircut:** [McLean and Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf) find anomaly returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication. Assume 1 to 2% a year excess, an information ratio near 0.2, so R500 to R1,000.
- **Vehicle:** Satrix Momentum (TER 0.40% on the [June 2024 factsheet](https://satrix.co.za/fund/mdd/STXMMT)) is cheaper than ten hand-traded shares paying STT and spread.
- **Small-player edge:** none beyond patience.
- **Test:** none. An information ratio of 0.2 needs (2/0.2)^2 = 100 years to reach t = 2, and free .JO data is survivorship-biased. Accept the published prior or skip it. Do not pretend to validate it.

**3. SENS event drift in small caps (E18; paper only for now)**
- **Evidence is mixed.**
  - [Cata (UCT)](https://open.uct.ac.za/handle/11427/15475): trading statements move prices on the day, with no significant drift afterwards.
  - Swart and Hoffman 2013, as summarised in my search: drift from about day 20 to 40, not shown to be exploitable after costs.
  - [Gallagher (UCT)](https://open.uct.ac.za/handle/11427/22916): significant 20-day abnormal returns after directors' trades, larger in small firms.
  - [Mokale (UP)](https://repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/24877): 0.43%, economically insignificant.
  - [Moonilal et al.](https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/view/5675/3618): the effect is concentrated in small companies.
- **Expected Sharpe:** 0.3 to 0.5 if real; R500 to R2,000 on a R20,000 sleeve before costs.
- **Small-player edge:** this is the only real capacity edge on the list, because funds cannot trade names where R5,000 is a normal ticket.
- **Costs:** small-cap round trips probably cost 2 to 3% (my estimate). Against a 2 to 4% effect, that fails your own 3x hurdle.
- **Data:** SENS via Sharenet or Moneyweb, subject to their terms.
- **Test:** two pre-registered hypotheses.
  - Directors' purchases in companies outside the Top 100, 20-day hold.
  - Positive trading statements, held to the results date.
  - Both are benchmark-adjusted, entered at the next day's close, with measured spread deducted. Pass at Holm-adjusted t of 3 or more, net.
- **Time to evidence:** at a 2% effect and 12% event volatility you need about 324 events, roughly 12 to 18 months if there are 20 a month (an estimate). At 1% it takes over four years.

**4. Diversified slow trend on futures (E19 redesigned; IBKR paper only)**
- **Evidence:** Moskowitz/Ooi/Pedersen and Hurst/Ooi/Pedersen as above. Secondary write-ups put gross Sharpe near 1.0 historically and SG Trend at 0.28 since 2000. AQR's [You Can't Always Trend When You Want](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/You-Cant-Always-Trend-When-You-Want) blames the weak 2010s on fewer large moves, not a broken signal.
- **Expected Sharpe:** 0.3 to 0.5 net.
- **Small-player edge:** none; this is a commodity strategy.
- **Capital:** a [second-hand summary of Carver](https://thealgorithmicadvantage.substack.com/p/033-rob-carver-the-comprehensive) suggests about USD 100,000 for a diversified book. Real money is years away, so the output is a record.
- **Data:** free ETF proxies across equities, bonds, commodities and FX. Yahoo continuous futures are unadjusted splices and will corrupt trend signals.
- **Test:** one trial, two slow EWMAC speeds combined (16/64 and 64/256 days), vol-targeted. The last 40% is the vault, which includes 2022, a known good trend year, so say so. Pass at vault net Sharpe of 0.3 or more. Count the earlier EWMAC trials in the deflation, since this is the same rule family.

## 2. Verdict on E18 and E19

**E18: keep it, but narrow it, and expect it to fail the cost hurdle.**
- Drop cautionary notices; their sign is ambiguous.
- Make the LLM a parser only. A trading statement states a percentage range, so extract it numerically and let a deterministic rule decide. An LLM judge adds untracked degrees of freedom.
- Use month one to measure events per month and real small-cap spreads, before any return is examined.
- Confirm your employer's dealing policy first. Pre-clearance and minimum holding periods can make 20-day holds impossible, and such policies often cover CFDs too, which touches the live cTrader account.

**E19: the EWMAC failure does not contradict the literature, because it was a different experiment.**
- CFD financing cannot explain a gross Sharpe at or below zero.
- The era explains part of it (AQR's finding above).
- The main causes are universe and speed. The 64 CFDs are mostly FX crosses that collapse to a few currency factors, with no bonds or rates and few commodities. Hourly-to-daily speed is far faster than the 1 to 12 month lookbacks in the published work.
- That is the materially different case: missing asset classes plus lookbacks of months. Move it off cTrader entirely.

## 4. What I would not do

- More hourly or intraday sweeps on CFDs. Cost is the binding constraint, and 29,000 trials have said so.
- Real money on cTrader beyond tuition, or IBKR micro futures with real money at this capital.
- Single-stock JSE backtests on Yahoo .JO data. Survivorship bias will manufacture an edge.
- An LLM as judge of anything that gets counted as evidence.
- Crypto carry, option selling, or leverage on EasyEquities.
- Paid data before a free-data forward test shows an edge.
- Crawling the JSE site against its terms.
- Trading bank shares or anything near your employer's information.
