# The Adaptive Trading Research Engine: build plan

This is the NEAR-TERM plan. The long-term architecture and direction, including the
principle that cTrader is Venue Adapter #1 rather than the boundary of the system,
live in `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

Written 2026-09-15. Supersedes the Phase 1 plan at `../.claude/plans/glistening-crunching-forest.md`
for everything from Phase 1b onward. Grounded in four research tracks run today, and in the
broker's own measured specs.

Owner decisions taken 2026-09-15: stay on cTrader FX and gold; autonomous within hard coded
limits; success measured as R1000 becoming R2000; risk mandate stated as capital preservation
first, which conflicts with the doubling goal at this account size (see section 2).

---

## 1. What the research says, in one page

Four tracks were run: LLM trading agents, automated alpha discovery and its statistics, news
feeds and world models, model agnostic agent engineering. The findings that change the build:

**Nobody has a verified record.** No audited third party verified track record exists anywhere
for LLM driven autonomous trading. The longest audited test of AI driven selection is AIEQ,
nine years live, +127.9% against SPY's +240.7%, with a deeper drawdown. The only real money
LLM experiment with public wallets saw five of six models lose, one finish at -99.4%, and fees
eat 6.3% of all capital in 17 days. In a successor contest, two winning entries were inverted
model signals. The bar in this field is on the floor, and the gap is not performance, it is
that no record exists at all.

**Search makes overfitting worse unless trials are counted.** The expected maximum Sharpe of
N pure noise strategies is E[max SR] = sigma * [(1-g) * Phi_inv(1 - 1/N) + g * Phi_inv(1 - 1/(Ne))],
g = 0.5772. At N = 1000 that is 3.26 sigma, so the best of a thousand coin flips shows a
backtest Sharpe near 1.63. A Sharpe 1.0 strategy found after 1000 trials needs roughly 15 years
of untouched out of sample data to be distinguished from that. Every rejected candidate counts
in N, including ones the failure memory skips.

**Failure memory is a density, not a veto.** Solved outside finance. TPE keeps failures and
selects on the ratio l(x)/g(x). Tabu search splits memory into auto expiring recency keyed on
move attributes, a permanent frequency counter that penalises rather than forbids, and an
aspiration rule that overrides a remembered failure when the candidate beats the incumbent.
MAP-Elites archives by behaviour so a weak result in an unexplored region survives.

**Reflection only works where a verifier exists.** Without an oracle, self correction degrades
accuracy (Huang et al., ICLR 2024). The engine already has the rare asset: a deterministic
walk forward plus Deflated Sharpe gate. Memory frameworks' own benchmark numbers are
untrustworthy; a plain full context baseline beats most of them.

**News at retail latency is unavailable.** One second of delay costs 7.3% of return with
perfect foresight; the winning races are 5 to 10 microseconds; last look makes release time
fills conditional on you not having been right, at 9 to 33% rejection. Pre announcement drift
died in 2015 and 2017. News enters as a slow macro state feature, never as a trigger.

**Do not build a world model.** Learned world models show roughly 40x higher error off path
than on path; structure halves it. Simulators are validated on stylised facts, and stylised
fact validation provably cannot distinguish structurally different models. Synthetic price
generators have a normality bias that erases the tails you would want to stress.

**Macro data must be point in time.** FRED serves latest revised vintages. ALFRED stores every
vintage with realtime_start and realtime_end. A macro backtest on FRED looks good and is wrong,
invisibly. This is free and non negotiable.

**Local models and fine tuning are premature.** Continuous A100 rental is about $1,500 a month
against 15 to 50 million output tokens for $50 of API. LoRA needs about 700 curated
trajectories as a floor and thousands to be competitive. The engine has 18.

## 2. The one open decision: the section 1 risk numbers

At 0.01 lots, one EURUSD pip is $0.10. A 30 pip stop risks $3.00, which is 5.4% of a $55
account. The minimum lot is a broker constant. Therefore:

* 1% per trade requires a $300 account. At R1000 the engine would decline every trade.
* Doubling requires accepting 4 to 9% per trade, with a material probability of ruin.
* Both goals become compatible at roughly R5000.

Recommended reconciliation, pending owner sign off: **capital preservation means a ruin floor,
not per trade smallness.**

```
RUIN_FLOOR        = R600 absolute. On breach the engine halts permanently and reverts to
                    research only. Plain code, no AI path, no override.
MAX_RISK_PER_TRADE= 5% of current equity, which is what the minimum lot forces.
MAX_DAILY_LOSS    = 10% of starting equity for the day.
MAX_OPEN_POSITIONS= 1. Margin at 1:100 does not support more at this size.
MAX_DRAWDOWN      = 40% from peak, measured daily, halts trading.
```

Until these are signed off, `risk/limits.py` stays fail closed and nothing places an order.
This is Phase 1d and it blocks live trading only, not any other phase.

## 4. Phase 1: the honest foundation

### 4a. Deep history
Replace 180 days of H1 bars with **Dukascopy tick data back to about 2003**, free and
unlimited, covering the majors and XAUUSD. This changes the sample from 3,000 bars to two
decades and is the single cheapest increase in statistical power available.

Keep recording your own cTrader ticks. That is the only record of your true spread, latency
and fills, and no external source can substitute for it.

### 4b. Point in time macro
ALFRED vintage API, keyed on `realtime_start`. Features: real yields, inflation surprises,
dollar index, volatility regime. Daily frequency. CFTC Commitments of Traders, timestamped at
the Friday release and never at the Tuesday as of date. Central bank RSS logged with both
publication time and fetch time.

### 4c. Validation that does not leak
* Purge and embargo: drop training observations whose label span overlaps the test span, plus
  an embargo of about 1% of the sample after the test span.
* Add **CPCV** alongside walk forward, for many backtest paths rather than one.
* Add **PBO via CSCV**. Hard fail above 0.5, proceed only below 0.2.
* Compute **DSR with an honest N**, where N counts every candidate the loop has ever scored,
  including skipped ones.
* Reserve a **genuinely untouched holdout** that the loop has never scored against, and record
  every time it is opened.

### 4d. Failure memory, rebuilt
Four levels of keying. Never place a permanent veto above the first row.

| Level | Key | Memory type | Decay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exact variant | hash(kind, params, symbol, period) | hard tabu | tenure T iterations, auto expires |
| Parameter neighbourhood | CVT cell over normalised parameter vector | soft density penalty | none, superseded by evidence |
| Strategy family | mechanism tag | frequency counter, score penalty | none, aspiration overridable |
| Hypothesis by regime | (mechanism, regime label) | per regime archive | rescored when the regime recurs |

Plus an **aspiration rule**: execute a tabu move anyway when it beats the incumbent best. This
is the mechanism that stops remembered failure becoming a blacklist, and its absence is exactly
the current bug.

### 4e. Regime layer
Use a **statistical jump model**, not an HMM. HMM dwell times are necessarily geometric, the
state count is not testable, and published regime charts use smoothed probabilities that are
not tradeable. Jump models reported Sharpe 0.48 to 0.68 and max drawdown -55% to -27% on the
S&P, and crucially survive a 10 day signal delay where HMMs do not. Use filtered probabilities
only.

## 5. Phase 2: the loop that actually learns

### 5a. Mechanism before search
Harvey, Liu and Zhu's own recommendation. A candidate does not enter the search without a
stated economic mechanism: who is on the other side, why they accept the loss, and why the
edge has not been arbitraged. The council writes it, deterministic code records it, and the
mechanism becomes the family key in the failure memory.

### 5b. Two stores, not one
* **Episodic**: append only trajectories, every proposal, decision, backtest and outcome, in a
  fine tune ready schema (messages, tools, outcome, reward). Costs nothing now and keeps the
  LoRA option open at about 3,000 trajectories.
* **Semantic**: distilled lessons, ACE style **delta updates with grow and refine**, never a
  full rewrite, which causes context collapse. Every lesson carries a **win rate** and is
  **evicted when it has not measurably helped in N trials**. Cap the injected playbook at about
  2,000 tokens and retrieve top k. Without eviction this is just accumulating text, and context
  rot makes that actively harmful.

### 5c. Scout
arXiv and SSRN, grounded citations only, no paper without a fetched resolvable identifier and
stored text. Map methods to mechanisms rather than directly to the existing six strategy kinds,
so the ladder can grow.

## 6. Phase 3: model agnostic, and cheap

**A gateway, not an SDK.** LiteLLM proxy with a `model_list` alias per role, so the code asks
for `planner` and YAML decides whether that is Claude, Gemini, a local vLLM or anything else.
Router level fallbacks, retries and cooldowns. The model never calls a tool directly: it emits
a call, the harness validates the schema, checks permissions, executes, injects the result.

What breaks on a swap, and must be contract tested per provider:
* JSON Schema dialects diverge. Design to the intersection subset and validate at the boundary
  regardless.
* `drop_params` silently deletes unsupported parameters including `response_format`, which
  downgrades guaranteed JSON to hope. Keep it off and maintain an explicit capability table.
* Reasoning token spend is not controllable across providers, so budget maths cannot assume it.
* Anthropic thinking blocks must be replayed byte identical with signature. Store the raw
  provider content array as the source of truth.
* Local models need a matching tool call parser per family, and grammar masking can suppress
  tool calls entirely. Two pass execution: decide unconstrained, format constrained.

**Cost control:**
* Append only context so cache prefixes stay unbroken. Cache reads are 0.1x, so break even is
  one hit.
* The nightly loop is an ideal **batch API** workload, which is 50% off at 24 hour SLA.
* Cheap model triages, expensive model escalates on low confidence or verifier failure.
* Per run: dollar budget checked before each call, wall clock timeout, max turns, and a no
  progress detector that aborts on an identical tool call with an identical result twice.

## 7. Phase 4: risk and live trading

Blocked on section 2. Once the numbers are signed off:

`risk/limits.py` becomes the Risk Officer. Plain code, hard limits, no prompt, no argument, no
override, no AI path. Fail closed. It halts itself on breach and cannot be restarted by the
loop, only by the owner.

**Graduation criteria, which is what "confident enough" means concretely.** All of these, not
some:

1. DSR above 0.95 with honest N, counting every candidate ever scored.
2. PBO below 0.2.
3. Survives on the untouched holdout, opened once, result recorded whatever it is.
4. A stated economic mechanism that survived red team.
5. Live execution telemetry shows realised spread, slippage and rejection within the tolerance
   the backtest assumed. This is what the account is for.
6. Minimum track record length satisfied for the claimed Sharpe, computed with the skew and
   kurtosis penalty.

Anything short of all six trades nothing.

## 8. Phase 5: the family interface

The dashboard already exists and is deployed. It needs to answer four questions in plain money
for someone who will never open a terminal:

1. How much is there right now, and how much of it is real versus simulated.
2. What did the engine do today, and if it did not trade, why not.
3. What has it learned, as lessons with win rates, not as a wall of text.
4. Is it still inside its limits, and what happens if it is not.

The "why it did not trade" answer is the most important one, because for most of this project
the honest answer will be that nothing passed the gate.

## 9. Phase 6: the record

This is the part with no competition. Publish timestamped, reconcilable execution and
prediction telemetry continuously: every prediction committed before the outcome, every fill
with requested versus filled price, every rejection, every cost. Six months of that would be
better evidenced than anything public in this field.

It is also the hedge. If the engine finds nothing, which the base rates say is the likely
outcome, the record is still a real contribution and the only honest one available.

## 10. What this plan deliberately does not build

* A world model, a market simulator, or synthetic price generation.
* Any strategy that depends on reacting to a news release faster than seconds.
* A local open weights model, until sustained usage exceeds roughly 50 million output tokens
  a month.
* LoRA or any weight update, until about 3,000 verified trajectories exist.
* Ubunye Engine integration. Its value is portability across many environments and connectors,
  and this system has one environment and one connector.
* Any claim of performance that is not produced by re runnable code against data the loop has
  not already scored.

## 11. Where things run and where things are stored (settled 2026-09-15)

| Layer | Location | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tick ingestion and feature ETL (Ubunye Engine, pandas backend) | This machine, `T:\making-money\ticks` and `\features` | Tens of GB. `C:` has under 20GB free, so the system drive is not an option |
| Permutation sweeps | This machine, results to `T:\making-money\sweeps` | Task parallel, milliseconds per backtest. No cloud beats an owned machine here |
| Daily light cycle | GitHub Actions | Free, 8 minutes a day |
| State the dashboard reads | Supabase `thabangvision-dev` (`bprargrqppufeuewryze`), schema `making_money` | Free tier, already running |
| Dashboard and chat | Render free web service | Free tier, already running |

**No cloud object storage.** Cloudflare R2 is in use by other projects and is deliberately not
used here. Artifacts produced on this machine stay on this machine. Only small summary rows
ever cross to Supabase, which is what keeps the hosted side inside the free tier.

**Schema isolation.** All six tables moved out of `public` into `making_money`, matching the
convention this Supabase project already follows (`multiversity`, `tutor`). Tables were moved,
not recreated, so the historical rows survived. PostgREST reaches the schema through the
`Accept-Profile` and `Content-Profile` headers, set from `settings.SUPABASE_SCHEMA`.
Requires `making_money` in Settings, API, Exposed schemas, or every call returns PGRST106.

## 12. Latency: what is needed and what is not (settled 2026-09-15)

**Real-time trading: not needed, and not attempted.** The strategies under test run on H1
bars. Section 1 already established that the sub-second game is unreachable at retail latency.

**Stream processing (PyFlink or similar): not needed.** The data rate is a few ticks per
second across eight symbols and a handful of deposits a year. That is not a stream processing
problem, and adding a cluster framework to it is the same category error as putting the
permutation sweep on Spark.

**How the engine learns the balance changed.** The broker pushes it.
`ProtoOATraderUpdatedEvent`, payload type 2123, fires whenever the trader record changes and
carries the full `ProtoOATrader`, including `balance` and `balanceVersion`. The connector
already holds the socket, so this is one message handler, not an infrastructure layer.

To build with it:
1. Handle 2123 in the connector dispatch and write the new balance through to the account
   snapshot, so a deposit is reflected within seconds.
2. Send `ProtoOATraderReq` (2121) at the start of every run and again immediately before any
   order, so position sizing never runs against a stale balance.
3. Read `leverageInCents` from that same message. Leverage of 1:100 is currently an inference
   from the `NOT_ENOUGH_MONEY` rejection of 2026-08-11. It should be read, not assumed,
   because the whole risk table depends on it.
