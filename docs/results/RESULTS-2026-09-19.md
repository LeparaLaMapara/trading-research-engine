## The answer in one paragraph

Nothing tested today is tradeable, and the reason is now known. On this broker, holding a
position overnight costs a fee (measured today from the broker's own swap quotes: about 1% a
year on FX, 0.8% on metals, **4.4% on major stock indices, 10% on crypto**, 6% or more on
energy). Across a diversified book that fee costs 0.4 to 0.6 Sharpe a year, more than any
signal we found earns. Trading costs, the thing we worried about last week, are small by
comparison at daily frequency (0.05 to 0.09). The limit is the venue, not the model.

## Findings worth keeping

1. **Pooling helps, as the literature says.** A single ridge model across all instruments
   beat one per asset class and one per instrument (gross 0.68 against 0.21 and 0.38). Most
   instruments have too little history to learn from alone.
2. **The neural "champion" was a long bet in disguise.** The GRU correlates 0.80 with simply
   holding everything; with that removed its skill is about zero. On the holdout it scored
   0.96 against 1.05 for holding everything. Always compare against the long-only baseline.
3. **There is a faint real relative signal in pooled trees.** XGBoost, with the long bias
   removed, earns 0.73 Sharpe before costs. Slowed to 10 trades a year it still nets only
   0.10, with a 95% interval of -0.64 to 0.78. Not distinguishable from luck.
4. **Carry was a wash, 2017-2024.** Interest earned (+0.58 Sharpe) was cancelled almost
   exactly by high-yield currencies falling (-0.58).
5. **Trend following did not work on this instrument set in any era, 2004-2024.** The
   published trend premium comes largely from bond, rate and agricultural futures, which this
   broker does not offer as continuous instruments.
6. **Assumed costs were wrong both ways.** Indices were undercharged (3% assumed, 4.4% real),
   metals overcharged (2.5% against 0.8%). Crypto longs pay 20% a year, shorts pay nothing.
   Re-scoring with measured fees changed no conclusion.

## What this means for the goal of growing capital

At this broker, no strategy found so far should receive money. That is not "try harder with
bigger models": the ML models already had signal before costs, and the costs are structural.
The highest value next steps, in order:

1. **Measure a cheaper venue for the same exposures.** Exchange futures carry no holding fee
   (only the implied rate, which is not a cost to a Sharpe calculation). VALR perpetuals
   charge funding instead of a fixed fee and have R10 minimums (see memory
   `sa-trading-access-reality`). Re-run the zoo's best candidates against those cost tables.
2. **Build continuous series from the broker's dated bond futures** (UST10Y, EURBND, JGB10Y,
   UKGB) and test trend on rates, where the literature says most of the premium lives.
3. **Intraday strategies that close before the 21:00 UTC rollover** pay no financing at all,
   but they pay spread many times over. Only worth testing where measured spreads are tiny
   (EURUSD, USDJPY, gold).

## Honest caveats

* Index CFD prices exclude dividends. Longs are credited dividends by the broker, which our
  price series misses (roughly 1.5 to 2% a year on indices), so index longs are understated.
* Financing fees were measured once, today, and applied to history. The fee is a broker term
  and fairly stable, but it has not been verified historically.
* Dukascopy is a different price source from the broker, and its bond and index histories are
  short.
